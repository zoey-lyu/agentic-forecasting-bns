# WTI Market Context — 2025-03-24

WTI crude oil prices have stabilized and ticked slightly higher, trading around $67.16 to $67.58 per barrel as of March 19–20, 2025. This represents a modest recovery and consolidation near a long-term support zone of $67.50. The recent upward drift is supported by a weakening US dollar and a larger-than-expected draw in US fuel inventories. However, the broader trend since the start of the year remains bearish, with prices pressured by macroeconomic concerns and expectations of rising global supply.

OPEC+ is moving forward with plans to begin unwinding its voluntary production cuts starting in April 2025, which has contributed to expectations of comfortable global crude balances later in the year. According to OPEC’s March Monthly Oil Market Report, OPEC-12 crude production rose by 154,000 barrels per day (bpd) in February, led by increases in Iran, Nigeria, the UAE, Iraq, and Saudi Arabia. Analysts note that the group's plan to gradually return supply to the market leaves little room for maneuver if non-OPEC production continues to expand robustly.

Geopolitical tensions in the Middle East continue to inject a risk premium into the market. Investors are closely monitoring ongoing US airstrikes against Houthi rebels in Yemen—marking the largest US military operation in the region since the new administration took office—as well as Israel's resumed ground operations in Gaza. While these conflicts have disrupted Red Sea shipping and raised freight costs, the direct threat to major oil transit chokepoints like the Strait of Hormuz remains a primary concern for supply security.

The global demand outlook presents a mixed picture. In the US, the demand environment remains healthy; recent Energy Information Administration (EIA) data revealed a substantial 2.8 million-barrel draw in distillate inventories, far exceeding analyst expectations, even though commercial crude stocks rose by 1.7 million barrels. Meanwhile, China has introduced a "special action plan" to stimulate domestic consumption and combat deflationary pressures, offering some optimism for demand recovery. However, global demand growth is still clouded by escalating trade tensions and the potential impact of new US tariffs.

In recent forecasts, the US EIA raised its WTI spot price projections, estimating an average of $70.68 per barrel for 2025 and $64.97 per barrel for 2026. Wood Mackenzie revised its 2025 Brent crude forecast slightly downward to $73 per barrel, pointing to the dual influences of OPEC+ supply plans and US tariff policies. Additionally, J.P. Morgan projected WTI to average $69 per barrel in 2025 before dropping to $57 per barrel in 2026, while BMI (Fitch Solutions) expects a more resilient average of $73 per barrel for WTI in 2025.
