As of December 11, 2025, West Texas Intermediate (WTI) crude oil prices are under significant downward pressure, settling at $57.60 per barrel, down approximately 1.5% on the day and testing multi-week lows. Global benchmark Brent crude settled near $61.28 per barrel. WTI has plunged more than 5.7% from its December highs, struggling to reclaim the $60.00 psychological threshold. The market is currently testing a critical technical support zone between $56.83 and $57.20, with the broader technical trend remaining firmly weighted to the downside.

The OPEC+ alliance is executing its previously agreed production adjustments, which included a modest 137,000 barrels per day (bpd) output increase for December 2025. However, to counter seasonal demand weakness and maintain market stability, the eight core participating nations—including Saudi Arabia, Russia, and the UAE—have agreed to pause any further production increments for January, February, and March 2026. While OPEC remains optimistic about demand, projecting global oil demand growth of 1.3 million bpd in 2025 and 1.4 million bpd in 2026, its view contrasts with other energy agencies warning of a looming global supply surplus.

Geopolitical developments have injected volatility into the market, though their upward price impact remains largely capped by bearish fundamentals. Tensions escalated following the U.S. military's interception and seizure of a sanctioned Venezuelan oil tanker, an action Caracas condemned as "piracy". Additionally, Ukraine has expanded its targeting of Russian energy infrastructure, recently striking Lukoil's Filanovsky oil field in the Caspian Sea. Conversely, ongoing diplomatic efforts and potential de-escalation in the Russia-Ukraine conflict are viewed as bearish factors, as a peace agreement could eventually pave the way for more Russian crude to enter global markets.

The demand outlook remains highly contested amid rising global stockpiles. In its December 11, 2025 Oil Market Report, the International Energy Agency (IEA) projected global oil demand to rise by 830,000 bpd in 2025 and upgraded its 2026 growth forecast to 860,000 bpd. However, the IEA also highlighted that global observed inventories reached four-year highs of 8,030 million barrels in October. Domestically, the U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA) reported an unexpected crude inventory build of 2.8 million barrels for the week ending December 5, marking the third consecutive weekly increase and underscoring that supply is outpacing downstream demand.

Market analysts maintain a cautious to bearish outlook for the near term. Technical strategists at City Index note that a sustained break below the $56.83 support level could trigger the next major leg down for WTI. Senior energy traders at CIBC Private Wealth Group observe that a weak macroeconomic equity backdrop and steady geopolitical headlines are keeping market sentiment soft, with only marginal short-covering triggered by the Venezuelan tanker seizure. Looking ahead to 2026, the EIA's latest Short-Term Energy Outlook forecasts Brent spot prices to average around $55.08 per barrel, reflecting expectations that expanding global production and rising inventories will continue to weigh on prices.
