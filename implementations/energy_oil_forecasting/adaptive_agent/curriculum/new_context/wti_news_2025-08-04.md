# WTI Market Context — 2025-08-04

WTI crude oil prices experienced significant volatility during the final week of July 2025. After settling at a multi-week low of $65.04 per barrel on Friday, July 25, WTI staged a sharp early-week rally, rising to $66.71 on Monday and surging past $69.00 to reach $69.28 by Wednesday, July 30 (with some postings hitting $70.00). This upward momentum was slightly capped on Thursday, July 31, as prices pulled back by about 1.1% to close at $69.26. The week's trading reflected a tug-of-war between immediate geopolitical risk premiums and bearish domestic inventory data.

OPEC+ continues to execute its strategy of gradually unwinding voluntary production cuts. Following a 411,000 barrels per day (bpd) output increase in July, the eight participating OPEC+ nations—including Saudi Arabia and Russia—agreed during their July 5 meeting to implement a further production increase of 548,000 bpd for August 2025. The group's next virtual meeting is scheduled for August 3 to determine September production levels. Meanwhile, OPEC’s latest Monthly Oil Market Report indicated that the group's crude production rose by 263,000 bpd in July to average 27.54 million bpd, driven primarily by increased output from Saudi Arabia.

Geopolitical developments heavily influenced market sentiment this week. Oil prices surged early in the week following President Trump's announcement of a drastically shortened 10-to-12-day timeline for Russia to broker peace in Ukraine, down from the previously expected 50 days. Trump threatened severe secondary sanctions, including 100% tariffs on any nation importing Russian crude, which would heavily impact major buyers like China and India. Because Russia exports over 7 million bpd, analysts warned that any actual disruption would completely erase the projected global surplus through 2026. However, some of these supply fears eased later in the week as progress was reported in U.S.-Iran talks regarding a nuclear framework.

The demand outlook received a long-term boost from a transatlantic trade agreement between the U.S. and the European Union, which halved potential export tariffs to 15% and included an EU commitment to purchase $750 billion in U.S. energy products over the coming years. Despite this, near-term demand concerns persist; the IEA's July report projected global oil demand growth of just 700,000 bpd for 2025, while OPEC maintained a more optimistic forecast of 1.3 million bpd. On the inventory front, the EIA reported a massive, unexpected build in U.S. commercial crude stocks, which rose by 7.7 million barrels to 426.7 million barrels for the week ending July 25. This surge far exceeded market expectations of a draw, though its bearish impact was temporarily overshadowed by geopolitical headlines.

Market analysts remain divided on the medium-to-long-term price trajectory. The U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA) recently revised its 2025 Brent price forecast upward to $69 per barrel due to near-term geopolitical risks, but still projects a decline to $58 per barrel in 2026 as global supply growth outpaces demand. Neil Crosby of Sparta Commodities warned that if secondary sanctions are avoided, the combination of soft global demand and rising OPEC+ exports post-summer will likely lead to further inventory builds and weaker prices. Conversely, GLJ maintained a fundamentally grounded long-term real price forecast for WTI at $72.00 per barrel, reflecting confidence in the durability of global supply-demand balances despite short-term volatility.
