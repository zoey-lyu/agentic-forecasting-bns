West Texas Intermediate (WTI) crude oil prices are experiencing a distinct bearish trend, trading in the low $60s per barrel. During the final week of April 2025, WTI spot prices at Cushing, Oklahoma, fluctuated between $59.55 and $63.30 per barrel, closing at $60.59 on May 1. This downward trajectory follows a sharp sell-off triggered by the U.S. administration's sweeping tariff announcements on April 2, which initially dragged WTI down to a low of $55.12 on April 9. Technical analysts note that WTI faces immediate resistance around the $65.00 to $65.10 range, with key support levels established at $61.50 and $58.40. A failure to hold these support levels could trigger a deeper correction toward the mid-to-low $50s.

On the supply side, OPEC+ is moving forward with its strategy to unwind 2.2 million barrels per day (b/d) of voluntary production cuts, which officially commenced on April 1, 2025. Following a virtual meeting on April 3, the eight core participating nations—including Saudi Arabia, Russia, and the UAE—reaffirmed their commitment to a gradual return of supply. The group agreed to implement a substantial production increase of 411,000 b/d in May 2025, effectively rolling three planned monthly increments into one. While this decision signals a rapid return of barrels, OPEC+ has emphasized a flexible approach, retaining the option to pause or reverse these hikes if market conditions deteriorate. The coalition is scheduled to meet next on May 5, 2025, to determine June production quotas.

Geopolitical supply risks are currently dominated by escalating global trade tensions rather than direct physical disruptions. The threat of a full-scale trade war, sparked by U.S. tariff proposals on major trading partners including China, the EU, Canada, and Mexico, has introduced severe volatility and recessionary fears into the energy markets. While energy imports have largely been exempted from direct tariffs, the broader economic fallout is expected to dampen industrial activity. Meanwhile, ongoing U.S. sanctions on Russia, Iran, and Venezuela continue to restrict some flows, but these supply-side constraints are being overshadowed by the combination of rising OPEC+ output and non-OPEC production growth from the U.S., Canada, and Guyana.

The global demand outlook has been heavily downgraded due to these macroeconomic headwinds. In its April 2025 Oil Market Report, the International Energy Agency (IEA) slashed its 2025 global oil demand growth forecast by 300,000 b/d to just 730,000 b/d, citing trade tensions. OPEC similarly revised its 2025 demand growth projection downward by 100,000 b/d to 1.3 million b/d. In its mid-April Short-Term Energy Outlook, the U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA) lowered its 2025 WTI price forecast to an average of $63.88/bbl, warning of a growing supply surplus. Analysts broadly agree that unless trade disputes are resolved or OPEC+ halts its production hikes, the market is headed toward a prolonged period of oversupply that will keep downward pressure on crude benchmarks.
