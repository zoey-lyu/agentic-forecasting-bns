During the week ending April 17, 2025, West Texas Intermediate (WTI) crude oil futures staged a strong recovery, reversing a multi-week downtrend. WTI began the week under pressure, trading near $60.74 per barrel on April 11 and dipping to a session low of $60.07 on April 14 amid oversupply concerns and tariff anxieties. However, prices rebounded sharply in the latter half of the holiday-shortened week. WTI rose 1.9% on April 16 to settle at $62.47 and surged another 3.5% on April 17 to close at $64.68 per barrel. Brent crude similarly rallied to settle at $67.96 per barrel. Both benchmarks finished the week approximately 5% higher, marking their first weekly gains in three weeks.

The supply side of the market is navigating the phased unwinding of OPEC+ voluntary production cuts. Following a virtual meeting on April 3, the "Voluntary Eight" OPEC+ members (led by Saudi Arabia and Russia) reaffirmed their commitment to gradually return 2.2 million barrels per day (b/d) to the market. The group surprised market participants by announcing a larger-than-expected production target increase of 411,000 b/d for May 2025, accelerating their planned timeline. However, during the week of April 11–17, OPEC confirmed it had received updated compensation schedules from overproducing members, including Iraq and Kazakhstan, who pledged to deepen cuts to offset past excess output. Analysts note that OPEC+ maintains the flexibility to pause or reverse these supply increases if macroeconomic conditions deteriorate.

Geopolitical developments and tightening sanctions provided significant upward momentum to oil prices late in the week. On April 16, the United States announced fresh sanctions targeting Chinese independent "teapot" refiners and other entities involved in moving Iranian crude oil, escalating pressure on Tehran during nuclear negotiations. This move raised immediate concerns over a potential reduction in Iranian exports, which could tighten global supply. Additionally, ongoing Western sanctions on Russian and Venezuelan energy sectors continue to disrupt traditional trade flows and force a reshuffling of global supply chains.

Despite the late-week price rally, the medium-term demand outlook remains heavily clouded by trade tensions and tariff policies. In its April 15 Oil Market Report, the International Energy Agency (IEA) downgraded its 2025 global oil demand growth forecast by 300,000 b/d to 730,000 b/d, citing the economic fallout of escalating trade disputes. OPEC also lowered its 2025 demand growth projection to 1.3 million b/d. Souring sentiment has been driven by U.S. tariff policies, though late-week optimism regarding a potential U.S.-EU trade deal offered some demand relief. Consequently, major institutions—including Goldman Sachs, JPMorgan, and the IEA—have trimmed their oil demand and price forecasts. Some analysts project a potential global crude surplus of up to 950,000 b/d in 2025, with HSBC forecasting 2025 averages of $62 per barrel for WTI and $65 per barrel for Brent.
