WTI crude oil futures are currently trading in the $63 to $64 range, with WTI settling at $64.07 per barrel on August 25, dipping to $63.25 on August 26, and rebounding 1.4% on August 27 to settle above $64 a barrel. Brent crude has hovered in the $67 to $68 range. While prices have been largely rangebound this month, WTI recently broke out above a key resistance level of $63.00, driven by tightening prompt physical spreads and a supportive technical structure, though it remains capped by a broader bearish channel.

The market is closely monitoring OPEC+ as the alliance continues to unwind its voluntary production cuts. Eight key member states—including Saudi Arabia, Russia, and the UAE—agreed to a partial rollback of voluntary cuts, boosting production by 548,000 barrels per day (bpd) starting in August 2025. This is part of a phased 18-month plan to restore 2.2 million bpd of supply by September 2026, though 3.66 million bpd in separate baseline cuts remain in place. Traders are also anticipating the upcoming weekend OPEC+ meeting, with market chatter suggesting potential further quota adjustments.

Geopolitical risks continue to support the market's risk premium. Recent Ukrainian drone strikes on Russian energy infrastructure, including a major fuel export terminal at Ust-Luga, have heightened fears of supply disruptions. Additionally, geopolitical tensions are complicated by U.S. foreign policy; President Donald Trump recently threatened sanctions on Russia if peace talks do not progress, and the U.S. imposed a 50% tariff on certain Indian goods to penalize India for purchasing Russian crude. Ongoing friction in the Middle East and potential threats to transit through the Strait of Hormuz also keep supply risks elevated.

The near-term demand outlook received a boost from U.S. inventory data. The Energy Information Administration (EIA) reported a larger-than-expected 2.4 million barrel draw in national crude stockpiles, alongside the first weekly inventory decline at the Cushing, Oklahoma hub in eight weeks. However, the longer-term demand outlook remains clouded by macroeconomic headwinds, including trade tariffs and weaker consumption growth in China. On the macroeconomic front, sentiment has been supported by Federal Reserve Chair Jerome Powell's signal of a potential interest rate cut in September, which could bolster global economic activity and fuel demand.

In a notable forecast released on August 27, Goldman Sachs projected that Brent crude could fall to the low $50s per barrel by late 2026. The bank expects a widening global oil surplus averaging 1.8 million bpd from late 2025 through 2026, which could swell global inventories by 800 million barrels. Meanwhile, technical analysts suggest that while WTI's breakout above $63.00 is bullish in the short term, the commodity faces strong resistance near $65.13, with potential pullbacks expected to test support levels between $63.00 and $63.80.
