# WTI Market Context — 2025-05-19

During the week of May 9 to May 15, 2025, West Texas Intermediate (WTI) crude oil prices experienced a highly volatile trading range, ultimately settling near $61.05 per barrel on May 15. Early in the week, WTI rebounded from its April lows of around $56.00 to hit a two-week high of $63.67 on May 13. This early-week rally was fueled by a temporary 90-day trade truce between the US and China, which significantly scaled back reciprocal tariffs and eased immediate global recession fears. However, these gains were erased later in the week due to bearish inventory data and shifting geopolitical developments. On May 14, the US Energy Information Administration (EIA) reported an unexpected commercial crude inventory build of 3.5 million barrels for the week ending May 9, defying analyst expectations of a 1.1 million-barrel draw and highlighting immediate oversupply pressures.

The market continues to digest the impact of OPEC+’s decision to accelerate the unwinding of its voluntary production cuts, which included a 411,000 barrels per day (bpd) output hike in May and another planned 411,000 bpd increase for June. In its monthly report released on May 14, OPEC trimmed its forecast for non-OPEC+ supply growth by 100,000 bpd for both 2025 and 2026, though it maintained its global demand growth projections. Conversely, the International Energy Agency (IEA) in its May 15 report raised its 2025 global supply growth forecast by 380,000 bpd to 1.6 million bpd, noting that Saudi Arabia is poised to implement the bulk of these increases due to quota non-compliance among other member states.

Geopolitical risk premiums fluctuated rapidly throughout the week. While early-week prices were supported by potential US sanctions on Venezuelan crude and persistent Middle East tensions, these concerns were overshadowed on May 15 by comments from US President Donald Trump. Trump announced that the US was in "very serious negotiations" with Iran regarding its nuclear program, signaling a potential long-term peace deal. Analysts warned that a successful agreement could lift sanctions and unleash an additional 200,000 to 300,000 bpd of Iranian crude onto an already saturated global market, compounding the pressure from Iran's existing exports, which reached 1.7 million bpd in April.

The broader demand outlook remains constrained by macroeconomic headwinds and rising electric vehicle (EV) adoption. In its May 15 Oil Market Report, the IEA slightly upgraded its full-year 2025 global oil demand growth forecast by 20,000 bpd to 740,000 bpd due to lower prices, but warned that growth will slow to a subdued 650,000 bpd for the remainder of the year. This creates a stark imbalance against the projected 1.6 million bpd global supply growth. Market analysts, including those at Macquarie and Again Capital, noted that while the US-China trade truce established a temporary price floor for WTI near $60 per barrel, the combination of rising OPEC+ output, potential Iranian barrels, and slowing demand growth will likely cap any sustained upward momentum.
