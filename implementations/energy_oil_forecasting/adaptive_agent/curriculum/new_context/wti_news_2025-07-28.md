WTI crude oil prices traded in a relatively narrow range during the week of July 18–24, 2025, with prompt prices hovering in the mid-to-high $60s. WTI slipped slightly to $67.51 per barrel on July 18, before settling at $67.20 on July 21, $66.40 on July 23, and $66.03 on July 24. While prices found some mid-week support from a sharper-than-expected decline in U.S. crude inventories and optimism surrounding U.S. trade negotiations, the benchmark has struggled to break above its 200-day moving average of approximately $66.20.

On the supply side, OPEC+ is moving forward with plans to gradually unwind its voluntary production cuts. According to OPEC’s July 2025 Monthly Oil Market Report, the group's crude production rose by 263,000 barrels per day (bpd) month-on-month to reach 27.54 million bpd. This aligns with the broader strategy of eight key OPEC+ members—including Saudi Arabia, Russia, and Iraq—who agreed to accelerate output by 411,000 bpd starting in July 2025 as part of an 18-month phase-out of 2.2 million bpd in voluntary cuts.

Geopolitical developments continue to inject volatility into the market. In northern Iraq, repeated drone attacks on oilfields in the Kurdistan region disrupted between 140,000 and 150,000 bpd, cutting the area's output by more than half. Meanwhile, Western nations have intensified pressure on Russian energy exports. On July 18, the European Union agreed to its 18th sanctions package, which bans imports of refined products made from Russian crude in third countries and lowers the G7/EU crude price cap from $60 to $47.60 per barrel, effective September 3. As of July 24, Western allies have sanctioned a total of 535 Russian oil tankers to curb shadow fleet operations.

The global demand outlook remains highly contested between major energy agencies. The International Energy Agency (IEA) in its July report forecast exceptionally weak global demand growth of just 700,000 bpd for 2025—the lowest non-pandemic rate since 2009. Conversely, OPEC maintained a much more optimistic demand growth forecast of 1.3 million bpd for 2025. Near-term demand sentiment is also clouded by uncertainty surrounding U.S. tariff policies. From a technical perspective, analysts at Kase and Company suggest a bearish near-term bias for WTI, noting that a failure to sustain levels above $64.90 could trigger a downward test toward $63.90.
