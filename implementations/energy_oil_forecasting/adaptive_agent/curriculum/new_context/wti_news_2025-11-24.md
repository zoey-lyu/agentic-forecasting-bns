As of November 20, 2025, West Texas Intermediate (WTI) crude oil is trading in the high-$59 range, settling at $59.44 per barrel on November 19 and hovering around $59.66 to $59.67 on November 20. Brent crude is trading near $63.70 to $63.92 per barrel. Prices have experienced a downward trend over the week, punctuated by a sharp 2.1% drop on November 19. This decline was driven by a bearish American Petroleum Institute (API) report indicating a 4.45 million barrel build in U.S. crude inventories, alongside market rumors of a potential draft peace agreement between Russia and Ukraine that could ease sanctions and release more Russian barrels. However, the official Energy Information Administration (EIA) report released on November 19 countered some bearishness by reporting a larger-than-expected 3.4 million barrel draw in U.S. commercial crude stockpiles, though this was offset by rising gasoline and distillate inventories.

OPEC+ continues to navigate its strategy of unwinding voluntary production cuts. The group previously implemented a modest production increase of 137,000 barrels per day (bpd) for November and December 2025. However, to mitigate seasonal demand weakness in early 2026, the eight core OPEC+ member nations—including Saudi Arabia and Russia—have agreed to pause these scheduled production increases for January, February, and March of 2026. The wider group is scheduled to meet on November 30, 2025, to reassess market conditions and compliance.

Geopolitical supply risks remain highly active, particularly concerning Russian exports and Middle Eastern shipping lanes. Ukrainian drone and missile attacks have repeatedly targeted Russian oil refineries and port infrastructure, causing temporary disruptions such as loading delays at the key port of Novorossiysk. These attacks have severely tightened the European diesel market, pushing refining margins to multi-year highs. Furthermore, the market is bracing for a major U.S. sanctions deadline on Russian oil giants Rosneft and Lukoil, which are set to kick in within days. In the Middle East, shipping security concerns were reinforced on November 14 when Iranian forces seized a tanker in the Strait of Hormuz, highlighting ongoing vulnerabilities in the critical transit chokepoint.

The global demand outlook is characterized by a growing supply surplus. In its November Oil Market Report, the International Energy Agency (IEA) slightly upgraded its 2025 global oil demand growth forecast by 40,000 bpd to 790,000 bpd, bringing total demand to 103.88 million bpd. However, this growth is heavily outpaced by global supply, which the IEA projects will rise by 3.1 million bpd in 2025 and 2.5 million bpd in 2026. In terms of forecasts, the U.S. EIA's November Short-Term Energy Outlook (STEO) raised its WTI spot average price forecast to $65.15/bbl for 2025 and $51.26/bbl for 2026, predicting a downward price trajectory as global production outpaces consumption. Meanwhile, UBS analysts project Brent crude to remain range-bound between $60 and $70/bbl, with a year-end 2025 target of $62/bbl, noting that rising oil-on-water volumes have yet to translate into significant on-land inventory builds.
