West Texas Intermediate (WTI) crude oil prices have experienced significant volatility, settling at $62.27 per barrel on Wednesday, April 23, 2025, after a 2.2% single-day drop. This followed a brief rebound to $64.68 per barrel on April 17. The broader trend in April has been bearish, with prices hitting four-year lows earlier in the month due to fears that escalating tariffs and trade tensions between the US and major trading partners would sap global demand. Although prices recovered by over 15% from their sub-$60 lows following hopes of a US-EU trade deal and new sanctions on Iran, they remain under pressure, trading in the low $62 to $63 range as of April 24.

OPEC+ supply dynamics are adding to the bearish sentiment. The alliance previously confirmed it would gradually phase out 2.2 million barrels per day (b/d) of voluntary cuts starting in April 2025. Eight member countries, including Saudi Arabia and Russia, agreed to implement a production increase of 411,000 b/d in May. Downward pressure on prices intensified on April 23 following reports that some OPEC+ members might seek an accelerated output increase. Additionally, Kazakhstan's newly appointed energy minister initially stated the country could not reduce production at its three largest projects, though this statement was later walked back.

Geopolitical developments present mixed signals for supply. Fresh US sanctions targeting Iranian oil export networks have provided some price support by threatening to restrict flows. However, this is balanced by ongoing nuclear negotiations between the US and Iran, with a fourth round of talks expected to take place, raising the possibility of future sanctions relief and a boost in Iranian supply. Meanwhile, the temporary 30-day moratorium on energy infrastructure strikes between Russia and Ukraine, which began on March 18, has expired, though it previously helped stabilize the market.

The global demand outlook remains highly uncertain and heavily weighed down by macroeconomic headwinds. Both OPEC and the IEA recently downgraded their 2025 global oil demand growth forecasts, with OPEC cutting its projection to 1.3 million b/d and the IEA revising its forecast down to 730,000 b/d due to escalating trade tensions. Rystad Energy warned that a prolonged US-China trade war could halve China's oil demand growth to 90,000 b/d, creating a "perfect storm" for low investor confidence. In its April 2025 World Economic Outlook, the IMF projected that oil prices would decline by 15.5% in 2025 to average around $66.90/bbl, falling further to $62.40/bbl in 2026 as supply growth outpaces tepid demand. Domestically, the EIA reported a minor weekly increase of 0.2 million barrels in US commercial crude inventories to 443.1 million barrels for the week ending April 18.
