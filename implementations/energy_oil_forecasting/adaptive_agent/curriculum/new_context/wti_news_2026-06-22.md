**Price Level and Recent Trend**
West Texas Intermediate (WTI) crude oil prices have experienced a sharp downward correction, hitting fresh three-month lows as geopolitical risk premiums rapidly evaporate. After settling at $84.88 per barrel on Friday, June 12, 2026 (a 6% decline for that week), front-month WTI futures slid further to trade near $80.50 on June 15. By June 16, WTI was trading around $81.00 per barrel—nearly 30% off its 2026 highs—before settling at $76.60 per barrel on June 18. This downward momentum reflects a major shift in market sentiment as traders price in the resolution of the Middle East energy crisis.

**Geopolitical Supply Risks and the Strait of Hormuz**
The primary catalyst for the price collapse is a major diplomatic breakthrough in the U.S.-Iran war. Following an announcement by President Trump on June 14, an interim 14-point agreement was digitally signed with Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian on June 17, with the official Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) released on June 18. The agreement lifts the U.S. naval blockade on Iranian ports and fully reopens the Strait of Hormuz, which had been closed to most commercial shipping for over 100 days. Maritime tracking data from Kpler and Lloyd's List Intelligence confirmed that major shipowners (including Grimaldi Group, Cosco, Knutsen, and NYK) began transiting the strait on June 17 and 18. However, the International Energy Agency (IEA) warns that operational and political constraints, such as prolonged demining and unresolved transit arrangements, still present lingering downside risks to the pace of the supply recovery.

**OPEC+ and Long-Term Outlook**
In its Monthly Oil Market Report (MOMR) released on June 11, OPEC revised its 2026 global oil demand growth forecast downward by 200,000 bpd to 1.0 million bpd year-on-year, while raising its 2027 growth projection by 200,000 bpd to 1.7 million bpd. On June 18, OPEC also launched its flagship World Oil Outlook (WOO) 2026, projecting that global oil demand will reach 124 million bpd by 2050. OPEC Secretary General Haitham Al Ghais emphasized that "no peak" is in sight for oil demand and called for $17.7 trillion in cumulative oil sector investments through 2050 to prevent future supply shortfalls.

**Diverging Demand and Supply Forecasts**
The gap between major forecasting agencies regarding the 2026 demand outlook has widened significantly, though their projections for 2027 are beginning to align. In its June 17 report, the IEA downgraded its 2026 demand outlook by 700,000 bpd, now projecting a global demand *contraction* of 1.1 million bpd (averaging 103.3 million bpd) due to a massive 5 million bpd year-on-year plunge in Q2 deliveries. For 2027, the IEA expects a demand rebound of 2.0 million bpd and a supply surge of 8.0 million bpd to 110.3 million bpd as Gulf production normalizes. Meanwhile, the U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA) has also revised its 2026 outlook to a contraction of 1.1 million bpd (a 1.3 million bpd downward revision from its prior monthly assessment), but expects a stronger 2027 rebound of 2.5 million bpd. On the supply side, the IEA reports that global observed inventories fell by a sharp 143 million barrels (-4.6 million bpd) in May, warning that stocks could still hit historic lows before the market shifts to a surplus by late 2026.

From a technical perspective, market analysts note that WTI has broken down from its symmetrical triangle consolidation pattern and is testing its 200-day moving average support, with a descending wedge formation indicating that sellers remain firmly in control of near-term price action.
