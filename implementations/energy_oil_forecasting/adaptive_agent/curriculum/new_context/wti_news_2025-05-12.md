# WTI Market Context — 2025-05-12

WTI crude oil prices have turned convincingly bearish, shedding over 20% since early April to trade primarily in the $55–$60 per barrel range, marking their lowest levels in over four years. WTI closed at $58.29 per barrel on May 2, 2025, and touched a near-term bottom of $54.95 on May 5 before recovering slightly to settle around $58.07 on May 7. The market has also shifted into a contango structure for contracts from October onward, indicating that traders are pricing in expectations of a persistent, long-term oversupply.

This downward price pressure is heavily driven by OPEC+’s unexpected decision to accelerate the unwinding of its voluntary production cuts. In April, the alliance announced it would increase crude oil production by 411,000 barrels per day (bpd) starting in May 2025—nearly triple its previous monthly target of 122,000 bpd. This aggressive supply hike is part of a broader strategy to return nearly 1 million bpd to the market across April, May, and June. Analysts suggest Saudi Arabia is leading this policy shift to discipline non-compliant members exceeding their quotas, such as Iraq and Kazakhstan, while asserting market share against rising non-OPEC supply.

On the geopolitical and macroeconomic front, escalating trade tensions between the U.S. and China are dominating market sentiment. The U.S. implementation of a new tariff regime, including a 145% tariff on Chinese imports, has triggered retaliatory measures from Beijing targeting U.S. liquefied petroleum gas (LPG) and petrochemicals. While energy imports are largely exempted from direct U.S. tariffs, the broader trade war has stoked intense fears of a global economic slowdown and a contraction in Chinese oil demand. Meanwhile, domestic U.S. crude production continues to hover near record highs, further compounding the global supply glut.

The demand outlook remains highly fragile as recession fears and tariff-driven economic headwinds grind global consumption expectations lower. In response, major financial institutions have revised their price forecasts downward. TD Economics recently trimmed its 2025 WTI average forecast to $62 per barrel (down from $67), while Goldman Sachs projects WTI will fall to $58 per barrel by December 2025 and trade in the $55–$60 range through 2026. Additionally, the U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA) released its May Short-Term Energy Outlook, forecasting that Brent crude will average $66 per barrel in 2025 and drop to $59 per barrel in 2026 due to rising global inventories.
