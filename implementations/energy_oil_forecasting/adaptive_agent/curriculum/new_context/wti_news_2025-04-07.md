As of April 3, 2025, West Texas Intermediate (WTI) crude oil is trading under intense bearish pressure, hovering at approximately $69.52 per barrel. This represents a decline of over 3% from the previous close, with WTI trading below both its 50-day and 200-day Simple Moving Averages (SMAs). The downward momentum has accelerated rapidly due to a double-whammy of sweeping U.S. tariff announcements and a surprise decision by OPEC+ to speed up production increases.

In a virtual meeting on April 3, 2025, eight OPEC+ members—including Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iraq, the UAE, Kuwait, Kazakhstan, Algeria, and Oman—shocked the market by announcing they would triple the pace of their planned production hikes. Instead of the previously scheduled 135,000 barrels per day (bpd) increase, the group agreed to boost output by 411,000 bpd starting in May 2025. This front-loaded adjustment represents three monthly increments of their gradual rollback of the 2.2 million bpd voluntary cuts. While OPEC+ officially cited "healthy market fundamentals," the timing has added significant bearish pressure to a declining market.

Geopolitical and trade policy developments took center stage on April 2, 2025, when U.S. President Donald Trump announced sweeping "Liberation Day" tariffs, including a universal 10% tariff on all imports and secondary tariffs of 25% on countries importing Venezuelan oil. Although energy imports from Canada and Mexico are largely exempted, the broader threat of a global trade war has stoked fears of a global economic slowdown and a severe hit to energy demand. Meanwhile, U.S. sanctions on Russian and Iranian oil vessels continue to loom, but have not yet disrupted flows enough to offset the bearish sentiment.

The demand outlook has deteriorated rapidly as fears of a tariff-driven global recession weigh on industrial and transportation activity. This bearish sentiment was compounded by the U.S. Energy Information Administration's (EIA) weekly report on April 2, which showed a massive 6.2 million barrel build in domestic crude inventories, far exceeding analyst expectations of a 2 million barrel draw. High domestic production of approximately 13.58 million bpd and sluggish global demand, particularly from China, continue to point toward a comfortable supply surplus.

According to the International Monetary Fund's (IMF) Commodity Special Feature published on April 2, 2025, oil prices are projected to decline significantly, with futures markets indicating that oil prices will average $66.9 per barrel in 2025 (a 15.5% decline) before falling to $62.4 in 2026. Traders are closely monitoring key technical support levels for WTI at $68.50 and psychological support at $67.00, while immediate resistance is observed at $70.00.
