WTI crude oil prices have remained relatively rangebound in early July 2025, with August futures trading around $66.84 per barrel on the NYMEX as of July 4. This consolidation follows a period of heightened volatility driven by regional conflicts in the Middle East. While prices have faced downward pressure from broader macroeconomic uncertainties, near-term supply concerns have prevented a deeper sell-off, keeping benchmarks stable. For instance, Brent crude climbed back to $68.43 per barrel in early July, reflecting the persistent geopolitical premium supporting the wider energy complex.

In a virtual meeting held on July 5, 2025, eight core OPEC+ member countries—including Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iraq, the UAE, Kuwait, Kazakhstan, Algeria, and Oman—reaffirmed their commitment to market stability. The coalition agreed to implement a production increase of 548,000 barrels per day (bpd) for August 2025, continuing their gradual and flexible rollback of the 2.2 million bpd voluntary cuts initiated in April 2025. The group emphasized strict adherence to production targets and reiterated their pledge to fully compensate for any overproduced volumes since January 2024, with their next review meeting scheduled for August 3, 2025.

Geopolitical risks have re-emerged as a primary driver of market anxiety. In early July, oil prices found support after Iran restricted International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA) access to its nuclear sites, raising fears of renewed escalation in the Middle East. On the demand side, the outlook remains mixed; while positive U.S. employment data suggests a resilient domestic fuel consumption outlook, broader concerns over global economic growth and potential trade tariffs continue to weigh on long-term demand sentiment.

In its Short-Term Energy Outlook (STEO) released on July 8, 2025, the U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA) slightly revised its near-term oil price forecast upward due to Middle East unrest. Nonetheless, the EIA maintains that growing global supply will outpace demand, forecasting Brent crude to average under $70 per barrel in 2025 and fall to approximately $58 per barrel in 2026. Meanwhile, analyst Norbert Ruecker noted on July 10 that geopolitical price spikes are typically short-lived, with prices expected to return to baseline fundamentals within weeks.
