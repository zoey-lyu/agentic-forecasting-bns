WTI crude oil prices closed the first trading day of the new year on January 2, 2025, at $73.13 per barrel, representing a sharp 2% (or $1.41) daily gain. This rally built on a positive trend from the final days of 2024, where WTI rebounded more than 10% from its December lows to end the year at approximately $71.72 per barrel on December 31. For the full year of 2024, WTI prices remained virtually unchanged, down slightly by about 4%, marking a second consecutive annual decline amid comfortable global supply balances and a slowing Chinese economy.

The OPEC+ alliance continues to play a central role in managing global supply, having agreed in early December 2024 to extend its current voluntary production cuts through the first quarter of 2025. According to OPEC's December Monthly Oil Market Report, the group's crude production rose slightly by 26,000 barrels per day (bpd) in December to average 26.74 million bpd, driven by recoveries in Libya and Nigeria, which offset declines in Saudi Arabia, the UAE, and Iraq. Analysts note that a projected global supply surplus in 2025 leaves very little room for OPEC+ to unwind these cuts, with some forecasting that any supply increases may be delayed until late 2025.

Geopolitical factors remain a primary source of upward price pressure and market volatility. Traders are closely monitoring the transition to the incoming U.S. administration under Donald Trump, set to take office on January 20, 2025. Analysts expect the administration to resume a "maximum pressure" campaign against Iran, potentially tightening global supply through stricter enforcement of sanctions on Iranian and Russian oil exports. Additionally, ongoing conflicts in the Middle East and Ukraine, alongside Europe's efforts to secure alternative energy supplies following the halt of Russian pipeline gas transit through Ukraine, continue to inject risk premiums into crude futures.

The demand outlook is characterized by a tug-of-war between short-term optimism and long-term structural headwinds. The early January price rally was heavily supported by Chinese President Xi Jinping's New Year's address, in which he pledged proactive policies to stimulate economic growth in 2025, boosting hopes for a recovery in the world's largest oil importer. In the West, a severe winter cold snap across North America and Europe has temporarily lifted demand for heating oil and diesel. However, broader demand growth is expected to remain subdued; the IEA projects global demand growth to accelerate to 1.1 million bpd in 2025, while OPEC maintains a higher forecast of 1.45 million bpd.

According to a Reuters poll of 31 economists and analysts released on January 2, 2025, ample non-OPEC+ supply and weak Chinese demand are expected to keep oil prices constrained near $70 per barrel throughout 2025. The poll forecasts Brent crude to average $74.33 per barrel and U.S. WTI to average $70.86 per barrel for the year. Similarly, the Q4 2024 Dallas Fed Energy Survey, published on January 2, 2025, revealed that oil and gas executives expect WTI to end 2025 at an average of $71 per barrel, with individual budgeting forecasts ranging from $53 to $100 per barrel.
