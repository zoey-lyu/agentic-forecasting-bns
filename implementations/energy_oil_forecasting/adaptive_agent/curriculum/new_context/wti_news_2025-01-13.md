WTI crude oil prices started the first week of 2025 on a strong note, rallying to multi-month highs near $74 to $75 per barrel. This upward momentum was driven by technical buying and supportive near-term fundamentals. However, the rally faced strong technical resistance at the 200-day moving average and the psychological $75 level, prompting a minor pullback. By January 8, 2025, WTI front-month futures settled down at $73.32 per barrel. Despite the mid-week decline, market indicators show that U.S. crude inventories have decreased for the seventh consecutive week—falling by 959,000 barrels—marking the longest streak of drawdowns in three years and keeping physical supplies tight.

On the supply side, OPEC+ continues to exercise voluntary production restraint, having agreed in late 2024 to shift the timeline for unwinding some of its output cuts further into 2026. Compliance and tighter supplies are evident as Russia's December 2024 oil production fell below its OPEC+ target, with its seaborne exports dropping to their lowest levels since August 2023. Meanwhile, the U.S. continues to maintain its position as a leading global producer, with the EIA projecting domestic crude output to average 13.5 million barrels per day (b/d) in 2025.

Geopolitical tensions remain a persistent driver of the market's risk premium as 2025 begins. Ongoing conflicts in the Middle East and Ukraine continue to threaten supply stability. Additionally, new supply-chain frictions have emerged as ports in China's Shandong province—the primary hub for independent refiners—have been instructed to block U.S.-sanctioned tankers, complicating the flow of Iranian crude and forcing Chinese buyers to seek alternative grades in the Middle Eastern spot market.

The near-term demand outlook is heavily supported by a severe winter cold front across the Northern Hemisphere, which has boosted heating fuel consumption and raised concerns over potential production freeze-offs in the U.S.. JPMorgan Chase & Co. reported robust early January demand indicators, forecasting global oil demand to average 101.4 million b/d this month, a 1.4 million b/d increase year-over-year. Optimism is further bolstered by China's recent aggressive fiscal stimulus measures. However, long-term forecasts lean bearish; the EIA's January 2025 Short-Term Energy Outlook (STEO) projects Brent crude prices to fall from an average of $81/b in 2024 to $74/b in 2025 and $66/b in 2026, as rising non-OPEC+ supply from the U.S., Brazil, Canada, and Norway is expected to outpace global demand growth. Similarly, Eurasia Group expects balanced fundamentals but notes that low demand growth could be offset by expanding non-OPEC supply.
