WTI crude oil prices have experienced extreme volatility, driven by the escalating US-Israel-Iran conflict that began on February 28, 2026. On Monday, March 9, WTI surged by nearly 30% at the Asian open to hit a four-year high of $119.54 per barrel due to fears of a severe supply shock. However, prices plummeted by 35% on Tuesday, March 10, to an intraday low of $76.83 (with the April contract settling at $83.45) following remarks by US President Donald Trump suggesting the war could end soon, alongside news of a massive emergency stockpile release. By Thursday, March 12, WTI rebounded and stabilized, trading between $94.49 and $95.02 per barrel.

The conflict has triggered the largest supply disruption in global oil market history, primarily due to the near-complete shutdown of commercial tanker traffic through the Strait of Hormuz, which typically handles 20% of global supply. Consequently, Gulf producers have shut in or cut production by at least 10 million bpd as domestic storage tanks fill up. Iraq has slashed its output by roughly 60%, pumping between 1.7 million and 1.8 million bpd (down from 4.3 million bpd pre-conflict), while Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, and the UAE have also implemented steep cuts. In its March Monthly Oil Market Report, OPEC noted that Saudi Arabia had pumped 10.882 million bpd in February as part of contingency planning, though it only supplied 10.11 million bpd to the market. Non-OPEC+ producers, particularly the US and Canada, are expected to boost production to capitalize on the price surge.

Geopolitical risks remain acute as Iran's new supreme leader, Mojtaba Khamenei, declared on March 12 that the Strait of Hormuz would remain blocked. To counter this massive supply shock, the IEA and its 32 member countries unanimously agreed on March 11 to a record-breaking coordinated release of 400 million barrels of oil from emergency reserves—representing one-third of their total government stockpiles. The US will contribute approximately 172 million barrels over 120 days. However, S&P Global Energy CERA estimates that the supply reduction in March alone will reach roughly 430 million barrels, meaning the emergency release is viewed by analysts as a temporary "stop-gap measure" that cannot fully compensate for lost Middle East supply.

The demand outlook has softened as high prices and flight cancellations weigh on consumption. In its March 12 Oil Market Report, the IEA downgraded its 2026 global oil demand growth forecast by 210,000 bpd to 640,000 bpd, warning that Middle East disruptions will curb global demand by 1 million bpd during March and April. Conversely, OPEC's March report maintained a much more optimistic demand growth forecast of 1.4 million bpd for 2026. On the technical front, analysts at OANDA noted that WTI's bullish uptrend remains intact if it holds above its key short-term support level of $88.36, with potential targets at $102.25 and $116–$119. Meanwhile, FBS analysts cautioned that the $94.11 to $99.20 zone could act as strong resistance, and a failure to clear it could trigger a pullback toward the $77.61 swing low.
