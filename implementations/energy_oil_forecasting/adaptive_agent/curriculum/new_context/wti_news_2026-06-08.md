West Texas Intermediate (WTI) crude oil prices fell sharply, hitting their lowest levels in four months. On Monday, June 3, 2024, WTI crude dropped by 3.6% to settle at $74.22 per barrel. The downward momentum continued into Tuesday, June 4, 2024, with WTI easing another 1.6% (or $1.21) to settle at $73.01 per barrel according to Mansfield Energy, while other market reports cited a settlement of $73.25 per barrel. This represented a cumulative decline of approximately 8% over five consecutive trading sessions. The sharp sell-off reflected growing market anxiety that supply would outpace demand in the coming months.

The primary catalyst for the price decline was the outcome of the OPEC+ ministerial meeting on Sunday, June 2, 2024. The alliance agreed to extend its collective production cuts of 3.66 million barrels per day (bpd) through the end of 2025. Additionally, eight member countries agreed to prolong their voluntary cuts of 2.2 million bpd by three months until the end of September 2024. However, the group surprised the market by announcing a detailed schedule to gradually phase out these 2.2 million bpd voluntary cuts over a 12-month period starting in October 2024, at an initial rate of approximately 180,000 bpd per month. Although OPEC+ emphasized that this unwinding could be paused or reversed depending on market conditions, the prospect of returning barrels to the market triggered widespread bearish sentiment.

On the supply side, geopolitical risk premiums continued to fade as no major disruptions to crude flows materialized from ongoing conflicts. Meanwhile, non-OPEC production, particularly from the United States, continued to expand, adding to global supply cushions. Adding further downward pressure, the American Petroleum Institute (API) reported a substantial and unexpected build in U.S. crude inventories of 4.1 million barrels for the week ending May 31, 2024, alongside a similar 4-million-barrel surge in gasoline stocks, which far exceeded analyst expectations of a stockpile draw.

The demand outlook remained highly contested amid weak macroeconomic data. Recent economic indicators, such as a contracting U.S. ISM manufacturing PMI and sluggish industrial activity in China and Europe, fueled fears of a demand slowdown. While OPEC maintained a highly bullish forecast of 2.2 million bpd in global oil demand growth for 2024, Goldman Sachs projected a more conservative demand growth of 1.5 million bpd. Consequently, Goldman Sachs characterized the OPEC+ decision as bearish, warning that risks to their Brent price forecast of $75 to $90 per barrel are now skewed to the downside. Conversely, TD Economics maintained its 2024 WTI price forecast of $80 per barrel, arguing that the market would remain slightly undersupplied for the rest of the year before balancing in 2025, though they acknowledged downside risks from weak demand and rising non-OPEC supply.
