# WTI Market Context — 2025-10-20

WTI crude oil prices have plunged to multi-month lows, trading below the $60 per barrel threshold. WTI closed at $58.90 per barrel on October 10, 2025, and hovered between $58.51 and $59.40 per barrel by mid-week (October 13–15). This represents a decline of over 7.6% since the start of October and a drop of more than 13% from September highs. The downward momentum is heavily driven by a strengthening U.S. dollar, escalating trade tensions, and growing fears of a substantial global supply surplus.

OPEC+ has continued its strategy of unwinding voluntary production cuts, adding significant supply to the market. The "V8" group of OPEC+ members, led by Saudi Arabia and Russia, implemented a scheduled production increase of 137,000 barrels per day (bpd) starting in October 2025. This is part of a broader plan to gradually restore 1.65 million bpd of voluntary cuts. Analysts note that the alliance appears to be prioritizing market share over price defense. In its Monthly Oil Market Report (MOMR) released on October 13, OPEC maintained its global oil demand growth forecast for 2025 at 1.3 million bpd.

While Middle East geopolitical risk premiums have somewhat ebbed, trade policy has emerged as a major headwind for the market. On October 10, President Trump threatened 100% tariffs on Chinese goods, reigniting trade tensions between the world's two largest economies and oil consumers. Although working-level trade discussions resumed on October 13, the threat of tariffs and additional port fees has heightened fears of a global economic slowdown and reduced energy demand.

The demand outlook remains structurally weak, particularly in Asia, where Chinese demand is moderating and demand from Japan and China has hit six-month lows. In its October 14 report, the International Energy Agency (IEA) warned of a massive looming supply glut, maintaining its 2025 global demand growth forecast at a subdued 700,000 bpd. The IEA warned that the global oil market could face a surplus of up to 4 million bpd next year as OPEC+ and non-OPEC+ production (led by the US, Brazil, Canada, and Guyana) outpaces sluggish demand. Technical analysts note that WTI is testing critical support levels in the $58.19 to $58.46 range, with some market strategists projecting that WTI prices are likely to average around $56 per barrel by the end of 2025 due to the persistent supply glut.
