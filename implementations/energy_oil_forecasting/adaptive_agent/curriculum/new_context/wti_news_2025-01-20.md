West Texas Intermediate (WTI) crude oil prices surged to three-month highs in mid-January 2025, breaking out of the tight trading range that characterized the final months of 2024. After closing at $76.57 per barrel on Friday, January 10, WTI rallied sharply by $2.54 on Wednesday, January 15, to settle at $80.04 per barrel, before experiencing a minor technical pullback to close at $78.68 on January 16. This upward momentum represents a rally of over 20% from the December lows, supported by tightening physical market indicators and a drop in U.S. commercial crude inventories, which fell by 2.0 million barrels to sit roughly 6% below the five-year seasonal average.

The primary catalyst for the price surge was a major escalation in geopolitical supply risks. On January 10, the U.S. government imposed sweeping new sanctions targeting Russia's energy sector, specifically blacklisting major producers Gazprom Neft and Surgutneftegas, alongside 183 vessels linked to Russia's shadow tanker fleet. Analysts estimate these measures put approximately 1.5 million barrels per day (bpd) of seaborne Russian crude activity at risk, threatening to disrupt key export flows to China and India and forcing those nations to seek alternative supplies. Additionally, ongoing tensions in the Middle East and potential threats to the Strait of Hormuz continue to inject a persistent war risk premium into global benchmarks.

On the supply side, OPEC+ production decisions and compliance remain central to market stability. In its Monthly Oil Market Report (MOMR) released on January 15, OPEC maintained its global oil demand growth forecast for 2025. Data from the report showed that OPEC-12 crude production rose slightly by 26,000 bpd in December 2024, driven by output recoveries in Libya and Nigeria, while the core "Big 4" producers (Saudi Arabia, UAE, Iraq, and Kuwait) maintained strict adherence to their voluntary output cuts. This disciplined approach has left the alliance with substantial spare capacity to deploy if global demand conditions tighten further.

The demand outlook has received a seasonal boost from colder-than-normal winter temperatures sweeping across the Northern Hemisphere, which has significantly lifted consumption of heating fuels. According to the International Energy Agency's (IEA) January 15 report, global oil demand rose by a robust 1.5 million bpd in the fourth quarter of 2024—exceeding previous forecasts by 260,000 bpd. The IEA projects global demand growth to accelerate from 940,000 bpd in 2024 to 1.05 million bpd in 2025 as macroeconomic conditions marginally improve.

In notable analyst forecasts, RBC Capital highlighted that the new U.S. sanctions on Russian oil represent a "net addition to at-risk supply," introducing heightened volatility and uncertainty to the first-quarter outlook. Meanwhile, the U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA) released its January Short-Term Energy Outlook, projecting that Brent crude would average $74 per barrel in 2025 due to rising non-OPEC+ supply from Canada, Brazil, and Guyana. However, the EIA explicitly cautioned that its price forecast was finalized prior to the January 10 U.S. sanctions announcement, meaning actual global supplies could end up tighter than initially modeled.
