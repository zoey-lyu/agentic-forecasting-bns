During the week of March 7 to March 14, 2025, West Texas Intermediate (WTI) crude oil prices showed signs of stabilization and a modest rebound after hitting a six-month low near $65 per barrel. WTI settled at $66.25 on March 11 and rose to $67.68 on March 12, supported by a bullish U.S. inventory report and oversold technical indicators. Despite broader macroeconomic growth concerns weighing on futures, WTI's prompt spread maintained a bullish, backwardated structure, signaling resilient near-term physical supply and demand balances.

On the supply side, OPEC+ is preparing to begin unwinding its voluntary production cuts. Following a virtual meeting on March 3, 2025, the eight key OPEC+ member nations reaffirmed their plan to gradually and flexibly return 2.2 million barrels per day (bpd) of voluntary cuts starting April 1, 2025. Meanwhile, OPEC's March Monthly Oil Market Report (MOMR), released on March 12, revealed that core OPEC production crept higher in February, with OPEC-12 output rising by 154,000 bpd, led by increases in Iran, Nigeria, and the UAE.

Geopolitical factors continue to inject volatility into the market. The Trump administration's signal that it is prepared to strictly enforce sanctions on Iranian oil production has provided upward price support. Conversely, expectations of a potential 30-day truce in Ukraine have raised the prospect of Russian crude flowing more freely, tempering bullish momentum. Domestically, the U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA) report on March 12 showed a smaller-than-expected crude inventory build of 1.45 million barrels, alongside a massive 5.74 million barrel draw in gasoline stocks, highlighting robust seasonal product demand.

The demand outlook remains mixed. In its March 13 Oil Market Report (OMR), the International Energy Agency (IEA) forecast global oil demand growth to accelerate to just over 1 million bpd in 2025, reaching 103.9 million bpd, with Asia and Chinese petrochemical feedstocks driving the majority of the gains. However, the IEA slightly trimmed its near-term growth estimates for Q4 2024 and Q1 2025 due to weaker-than-expected recent delivery data. Analysts remain cautious; technical forecasters note that while WTI is attempting a short-term double-bottom reversal, the broader trend still risks testing lower support levels near $64.80, while institutional forecasts from Macquarie project a full-year 2025 WTI average of $64.89 per barrel.
