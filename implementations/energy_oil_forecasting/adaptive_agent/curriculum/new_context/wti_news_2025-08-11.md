WTI crude oil prices have faced downward pressure in early August 2025, with front-month futures slipping to settle around $67.33 per barrel, while Brent crude hovered near $69.69 per barrel. This bearish momentum was triggered by a massive, unexpected build in U.S. crude oil inventories, which rose by 7.7 million barrels for the week ending July 25, 2025—the second-largest weekly build of the year. This supply surge, combined with a significant drop in U.S. crude exports, pushed prices down by approximately $2.50 to $3 in the opening days of the month.

Notably, Nigeria was absent from the production adjustment schedule due to persistent infrastructure constraints and underperformance. While the group reaffirmed its commitment to market stability, it emphasized that these monthly increases remain flexible and can be paused or reversed if market conditions deteriorate.

Geopolitical risks continue to inject volatility into the market, primarily centered on the future of Russian oil exports. Analysts are closely watching U.S. foreign policy, as President Donald Trump has maintained threats to impose secondary tariffs on buyers of Russian energy if a peace deal with Ukraine is not reached. Concurrently, fresh European Union sanctions have already pressured Indian state refiners to suspend their purchases of Russian crude, raising concerns over potential localized supply disruptions.

The demand outlook remains clouded by macroeconomic headwinds, particularly fears that escalating global tariff policies could stifle economic growth and weaken energy consumption. While seasonal summer driving has provided some support to domestic gasoline demand, broader crude demand is showing signs of cooling. In terms of market forecasts, Clearview Energy Partners noted that Brent trading near $70/bbl reflects a balance between the potential loss of Russian oil and a large rise in crude inventories in China. Meanwhile, technical analysts note that WTI's Relative Strength Index (RSI) has dipped near oversold territory at 34–35, indicating a potential near-term bottom despite strong bearish momentum.
