# WTI Market Context — 2025-06-30

WTI crude oil prices experienced extreme volatility during the week of June 20–26, 2025. Following U.S. airstrikes on Iranian nuclear facilities over the weekend of June 21–22, WTI temporarily spiked to the $78 per barrel range. However, this geopolitical premium quickly evaporated, triggering a sharp sell-off on Monday, June 23, and Tuesday, June 24. WTI plummeted by 9% on Monday and another 4% on Tuesday, hitting a multi-week low and testing a key support zone between $63.60 and $65.00. By Wednesday, June 25, prices staged a modest recovery of approximately 1.5%, with WTI hovering around $65.24 to $65.35 per barrel, supported by a fragile ceasefire and strong domestic inventory data.

Geopolitical developments in the Middle East heavily dictated market sentiment. Over the weekend of June 21, the U.S. military launched airstrikes using B-2 bombers and GBU-57 "bunker-buster" bombs against three nuclear facilities in Iran. This direct intervention raised immediate fears of a prolonged conflict and a potential blockade of the Strait of Hormuz, a critical maritime chokepoint responsible for transporting nearly 20% of global oil consumption. However, supply anxieties eased dramatically after a U.S.-mediated ceasefire was brokered between Israel and Iran. Although former U.S. President Donald Trump criticized both sides for early violations of the agreement, direct hostilities temporarily halted, significantly reducing the geopolitical risk premium.

On the supply side, OPEC+ is proceeding with its previously announced production adjustments. Eight member nations, including Saudi Arabia and Russia, implemented a combined output increase of 411,000 barrels per day (bpd) for June 2025 compared to May. In its June Monthly Oil Market Report, OPEC maintained its forecast for global oil demand growth in 2025 unchanged at 1.3 million bpd, bringing total projected demand to 105.13 million bpd. The group also projected stable global economic growth of 2.9% in 2025 and 3.1% in 2026, supporting their expectations of steady consumption.

The broader demand outlook remains a point of divergence among major energy agencies. While OPEC remains optimistic, the International Energy Agency (IEA) recently trimmed its 2025 global demand growth forecast to 720,000 bpd, pointing to weak second-quarter deliveries in the U.S. and China. Despite these macroeconomic headwinds, short-term U.S. demand signals have been robust. The American Petroleum Institute (API) reported a larger-than-expected 4.3-million-barrel draw in commercial crude stocks, which was followed by official Energy Information Administration (EIA) data confirming a 5.8-million-barrel decline. This second consecutive week of significant inventory draws has bolstered confidence in strong seasonal fuel consumption during the peak summer driving season.

Market analysts are closely watching how prices behave as the focus shifts back to fundamentals. Analysts at J.P. Morgan noted that global energy prices are moderating following the ceasefire, maintaining that their base case remains anchored by fundamentals indicating sufficient global oil supply. Technical analysts highlight that WTI's drop to the $63.60–$65.00 support zone represents a critical psychological and technical level. Some suggest that because oil was already on an upward trajectory before the recent geopolitical flare-up, prices could bottom out near this support zone and resume their upward momentum, especially given the lack of any major surprise production hikes from OPEC+.
