WTI crude oil futures for June delivery are trading in a range between $61.25 and $63.03 per barrel, while Brent crude is hovering between $64.11 and $65.72 per barrel. The market is experiencing a consolidation phase following a sharp decline in April, with WTI struggling to break above a key technical resistance level at $64.00. Technical indicators, including the 100-day simple moving average remaining below the 200-day average and a downward turn in the RSI and Stochastic oscillators, suggest that the path of least resistance remains to the downside, with potential support near $56 per barrel if the ceiling holds.

On the supply side, OPEC+ is moving forward with its plan to gradually unwind voluntary production cuts. Eight member nations—including Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iraq, and the UAE—are implementing a combined production increase of 411,000 barrels per day (kb/d) for May 2025. This adjustment represents an accelerated phase of their 18-month plan to reverse 2.2 million b/d of voluntary cuts by September 2026. Under these targets, Saudi Arabia's required production for May is set at 9.2 million b/d, while Russia's is 9.083 million b/d.

Geopolitical uncertainties continue to provide a floor for prices and inject volatility into the market. Traders are closely monitoring the slow progress of nuclear negotiations between the United States and Iran, as Iran maintains its stance on uranium enrichment and the U.S. holds a hardline position. Additionally, ongoing European Union sanctions against Russia and broader instability surrounding the Russia-Ukraine conflict keep supply risks elevated, offsetting some of the bearish pressure from rising global production.

The demand outlook remains highly polarized among major energy agencies. In its May 2025 Monthly Oil Market Report, OPEC maintained a bullish stance, keeping its 2025 global oil demand growth forecast unchanged at 1.3 million b/d. Conversely, the International Energy Agency (IEA) released a much more conservative forecast, projecting 2025 demand growth to average just 740,000 b/d due to economic headwinds and surging electric vehicle sales. The U.S. EIA sits in the middle, forecasting 1.0 million b/d of growth. Analysts expect the market to remain range-bound in the near term, with upcoming weekly U.S. inventory data from the API and EIA likely to dictate short-term price direction.
