WTI crude oil prices are trading in the low $60s per barrel, showing signs of consolidation and a modest rebound after hitting multi-year lows earlier in the spring. On June 2, 2025, WTI settled at $62.52 per barrel, climbing slightly to end at $63.37 per barrel by June 5, 2025. Technical analysis indicates that WTI has established a sideways consolidation pattern, breaking above a descending triangle formation with dynamic support holding firm around the $62.00 mark.

Over the weekend of May 31–June 1, 2025, eight key OPEC+ nations—including Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iraq, the UAE, Kuwait, Kazakhstan, Algeria, and Oman—agreed to increase their collective July production quotas by 411,000 barrels per day (bpd). This matches the 411,000 bpd supply hikes implemented in May and June, representing an accelerated unwinding of the 2.2 million bpd voluntary cuts announced in late 2023. While the coalition officially cited healthy market fundamentals and low global inventories for the decision, market analysts suggest the aggressive hikes are also intended to enforce compliance among members who have repeatedly exceeded their quotas, such as Kazakhstan and Iraq. The group is scheduled to meet again on July 6, 2025, to determine August production levels.

Geopolitical developments continue to provide a supportive floor for crude prices. Recent Ukrainian drone strikes on Russian refining facilities and hawkish rhetoric from Iranian officials regarding de-nuclearization talks have injected a risk premium back into the market. Additionally, anticipated U.S. sanctions on Russia and ongoing tensions in key Middle Eastern shipping corridors are keeping supply disruptions at the forefront of traders' minds, offsetting some of the bearish pressure from the planned OPEC+ supply increases.

The short-term demand outlook remains supported by strong seasonal factors, as refiners' crack spreads remain well above historical averages ahead of the peak summer driving season. However, the longer-term outlook is increasingly clouded by trade tensions, particularly between the U.S. and China, and structural shifts such as China's rapid transition to electric vehicles. A Reuters poll of 40 economists released on June 1, 2025, reflected this cooling sentiment, downgrading the 2025 WTI price forecast to an average of $63.35 per barrel. Furthermore, some major banking institutions are forecasting that WTI could average below $60 per barrel for the year as rising non-OPEC+ supply and the cartel's unwinding of cuts outpace global demand growth.
