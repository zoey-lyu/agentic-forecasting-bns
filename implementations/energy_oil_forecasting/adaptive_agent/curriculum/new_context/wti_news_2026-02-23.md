West Texas Intermediate (WTI) crude oil prices have experienced a sharp rally, settling at a six-month high of $66.43 per barrel on Thursday, February 19, 2026. This marks a significant rebound from earlier in the week when WTI hovered near multi-week lows around $62.64 to $62.82 per barrel. Brent crude similarly climbed, trading near $70.95 per barrel. The upward momentum was supported by a 4% surge on February 18, triggered by escalating geopolitical tensions and a substantial draw in U.S. crude stockpiles. The Energy Information Administration (EIA) reported that domestic crude inventories fell by 9 million barrels to 419.8 million barrels, leaving U.S. stocks approximately 5% below the five-year average for this time of year.

Geopolitical supply risks have taken center stage as the primary driver of the market's war risk premium. Tensions between the United States and Iran have escalated significantly, with the U.S. amassing a massive military presence in the Middle East—the largest buildup since 2003. President Donald Trump issued a warning that Iran has at most 10 to 15 days to secure a diplomatic agreement regarding its nuclear program. Analysts at RBC Capital Markets warned that the failure to resolve these core contentions tips the scales toward a military confrontation, which would severely jeopardize oil flows through the Strait of Hormuz, a critical chokepoint for roughly 20% of global oil supply. Additionally, the collapse of Russia-Ukraine peace negotiations on February 18 further amplified supply anxieties.

On the supply side, OPEC+ is navigating a delicate balance. While the alliance has maintained its production cuts, elevated crude prices have led some member nations to anticipate a resumption of supply increases starting in April 2026. In its February Monthly Oil Market Report (MOMR), OPEC kept its 2026 global oil demand growth forecast optimistic at 1.4 million barrels per day (b/d). Meanwhile, the International Energy Agency (IEA) reported that global oil supply plunged by 1.2 million b/d in January to 106.6 million b/d due to severe winter weather disrupting North American operations alongside outages in Kazakhstan and Russia.

The medium-term demand outlook and price forecasts remain highly divided among major agencies. The IEA's February report projects a modest global demand increase of 850,000 b/d for 2026, driven entirely by non-OECD economies, particularly China's petrochemical sector. Conversely, the EIA's February Short-Term Energy Outlook (STEO) presents a more bearish outlook, predicting that global production will outpace consumption later in the year. The EIA expects rising inventories to eventually drag prices down, forecasting Brent to average $58 per barrel and WTI to average $53.42 per barrel in 2026, viewing the current geopolitical price spikes as temporary.
