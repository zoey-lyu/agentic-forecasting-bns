WTI crude oil prices are trading in the high-$68 to low-$69 range, with May futures hovering around $69.49 per barrel and spot prices at $69.72 as of March 27, 2025. The market recently experienced a corrective rally, bouncing off support levels to consolidate within a broader descending wedge pattern that has defined price action since mid-2023. A primary driver of this recent uptick was a surprise draw in U.S. commercial crude inventories; the EIA reported a 3.3 million barrel decrease for the week ending March 21, 2025, defying expectations of a stock build and providing immediate upward momentum.

On the supply side, OPEC+ is anticipated to increase oil output for a second consecutive month in May to balance global supply and demand. Meanwhile, the IEA notes that non-OPEC+ production is projected to rise by 1.5 mb/d in 2025, led by the Americas, though this growth could slow if crude prices decline. Additionally, U.S. trade policy has introduced fresh supply dynamics; President Trump signed an executive order imposing a 25% tariff on countries importing oil and gas from Venezuela, tightening global supplies and providing a supportive floor for prices.

The global demand outlook remains mixed amid macroeconomic uncertainty. According to the IEA's March 2025 report, global oil demand growth is projected to accelerate to just over 1 mb/d this year, reaching a record 103.9 mb/d. Asia is expected to drive nearly 60% of these gains, heavily led by China's petrochemical sector. However, recent delivery data have underperformed expectations, prompting analysts to slightly downgrade growth estimates for the first quarter of 2025. Furthermore, there are concerns that escalating global trade tensions and tariffs could disrupt supply chains and dampen long-term demand.

Market analysts and technical indicators suggest a highly balanced but cautious short-term outlook, as WTI trades near its 50-period and 200-period Simple Moving Averages. Technical analysts point to immediate resistance at the 50% Fibonacci retracement level of $73.42, followed by $75.23, while key support rests at $68.50 and the recent swing low of $65.75. Some analysts warn that the medium-term trend remains locked in a descending channel, indicating potential downward pressure ahead unless WTI can decisively break through key resistance thresholds.
