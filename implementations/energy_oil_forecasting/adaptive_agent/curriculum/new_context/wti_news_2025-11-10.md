WTI crude oil prices have faced downward pressure in early November 2025, settling at a two-week low of $59.60 per barrel on November 5 and trading around $59.43 on November 6. This decline was primarily driven by a larger-than-expected build in U.S. crude inventories, which rose by 5.2 million barrels to 421.2 million barrels, far exceeding analyst expectations. However, the downward momentum has been partially offset by a sharp 4.7 million barrel draw in U.S. gasoline inventories, indicating resilient domestic fuel consumption, alongside ongoing refinery maintenance bottlenecks.

In a virtual meeting on Sunday, November 2, 2025, eight key OPEC+ member nations—including Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iraq, and the UAE—agreed to proceed with a modest production increase of 137,000 barrels per day (bpd) for December 2025. However, in response to mounting oversupply concerns and seasonal demand weakness, the alliance announced a crucial pause on scheduled output hikes for the first quarter of 2026 (January through March). This decision signals the group's intent to defend price floors and closely monitor evolving market balances.

Geopolitical tensions continue to inject a risk premium into the market. Recent Ukrainian drone strikes on Russia's Black Sea port of Tuapse disrupted refinery operations, temporarily halting fuel exports and knocking out an estimated 240,000 bpd of capacity. Additionally, new U.S. and UK sanctions targeting major Russian oil producers like Rosneft and Lukoil have raised questions about Moscow's future supply capabilities. On the demand side, the outlook remains soft; J.P. Morgan reported that global oil demand growth reached 850,000 bpd year-to-date as of November 4, falling short of its 900,000 bpd projection due to subdued U.S. travel and shipping activity.

Market analysts have adjusted their outlooks to reflect these shifting dynamics. On November 3, 2025, Morgan Stanley raised its Brent crude forecast for the first half of 2026 to $60 per barrel (up from $57.50), citing the OPEC+ first-quarter pause and the impact of Western sanctions on Russian oil assets, though the bank still warns of a substantial market surplus peaking in the second quarter of 2026. Technically, analysts note that WTI has been consolidating within a symmetrical triangle pattern around the $60 mark, with a potential break below this support threatening a retest of October lows near $55 per barrel.
