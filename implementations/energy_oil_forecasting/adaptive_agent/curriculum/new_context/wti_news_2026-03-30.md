**OPEC+ Production Decisions and Supply Disruptions**
The ongoing military conflict in the Middle East has triggered the largest supply disruption in the history of the global oil market. The effective closure of the Strait of Hormuz has choked off roughly 20% of global seaborne oil supply, which previously stood at around 20 million barrels per day (bpd). Due to the closure and rapidly filling storage, Gulf countries have cut total oil production by at least 10 million bpd. Although OPEC+ had previously agreed to resume output increases starting in April 2026, the closure of the strategic waterway has made it impossible for the group to deliver on this deal. To mitigate the severe supply shock, member countries of the International Energy Agency (IEA) unanimously agreed on March 11 to release an unprecedented 400 million barrels of oil from their emergency reserves.
