As of September 11, 2025, West Texas Intermediate (WTI) crude oil futures settled below $63 per barrel, erasing earlier weekly gains with a 2% daily decline. Since the beginning of August, WTI has remained range-bound, fluctuating primarily between $62 and $67 per barrel. The market is locked in a persistent "tug of war" as short-term geopolitical risk premiums clash with broader macroeconomic headwinds and rising global inventories. For the week ending September 5, WTI had dipped to $61.64 per barrel following weak economic data and a surprise build in U.S. crude stockpiles.

OPEC+ held a virtual meeting on Sunday, September 7, 2025, where the group decided to maintain its strategy of gradual production increases. The participating members approved a modest supply boost of 137,000 barrels per day (bpd) starting in October 2025. This follows a much larger 547,000 bpd increase implemented for September, which completed the unwinding of 2.2 million bpd of voluntary cuts. While the smaller October increment indicates a less aggressive approach to returning barrels to the market, the group remains committed to gradually phasing out its remaining 1.6 million bpd of voluntary cuts through 2026, though they retain the flexibility to pause or reverse these hikes if market conditions deteriorate.

Geopolitical tensions in both the Middle East and Europe continue to inject volatility and provide a temporary floor for prices. Recent escalations, including Israeli strikes targeting Hamas leaders in Doha, Qatar, and heightened airspace friction in Eastern Europe, briefly triggered short-covering rallies earlier in the week. Additionally, political maneuvers, such as U.S. President Donald Trump calling for 100% tariffs on EU goods from China and India to squeeze Russian oil revenues, have kept traders on edge. However, analysts emphasize that these events have not led to direct physical supply disruptions, and the geopolitical premium is increasingly being overshadowed by the reality of rising global supply.

The demand outlook remains heavily pressured by macroeconomic weakness and rising inventories. In its September 11, 2025 report, the International Energy Agency (IEA) marginally raised its 2025 global oil demand growth forecast to 740,000 bpd, but warned of a looming record oil surplus in 2026 as non-OPEC+ production expands alongside OPEC+ hikes. This bearish sentiment was compounded by U.S. economic data showing a surge in jobless claims and a surprise 3.9 million-barrel build in domestic crude inventories for the week ending September 5. In terms of forecasts, Fitch Ratings updated its assumptions on September 9, keeping its base-case WTI price projection at $65.00 per barrel for 2025 and $60.00 per barrel for 2026, reflecting expectations of slower global economic growth and a well-supplied market.
