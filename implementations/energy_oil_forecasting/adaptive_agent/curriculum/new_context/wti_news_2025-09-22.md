West Texas Intermediate (WTI) crude oil prices are trading in a tight, range-bound territory, hovering between $63.30 and $64.05 per barrel. On Monday, September 15, WTI closed at $63.30, holding steady at $63.32 on Tuesday before ticking up to close at $64.05 on Wednesday, September 17. Technical analysts note that WTI is testing a double bottom pattern neckline around $63.40, with key support established near $61.80. This range-bound "tug of war" reflects a market caught between near-term oversupply concerns and supportive macroeconomic and geopolitical factors.

On the supply side, OPEC+ is proceeding with its scheduled unwinding of voluntary production cuts. For September 2025, the group is set to increase production by 547,000 barrels per day, completing the rollback of the 2.2 million barrels per day of voluntary cuts implemented since November 2023. Despite this scheduled return of barrels, OPEC+ spare capacity remains high at approximately 4.6 million barrels per day. Meanwhile, the physical market is adjusting to new trade flows, notably with Nigeria's newly commissioned 650,000 bpd Dangote refinery ramping up its appetite for US WTI Midland crude while beginning to export gasoline to North America.

Geopolitical risks continue to provide a firm floor for prices. Ukrainian drone attacks on Russian energy infrastructure and refineries have intensified, raising fears of supply disruptions from Russia, which accounts for over 10% of global output. Additionally, persistent tensions in the Middle East and concerns over potential transit disruptions in the Strait of Hormuz keep a geopolitical premium priced into the market. On the policy front, US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent indicated that Washington would refrain from imposing unilateral tariffs on Chinese goods over its purchases of Russian oil unless European allies enacted similar steep duties.

The demand outlook remains a point of divergence among major forecasting agencies. In its September Monthly Oil Market Report, OPEC maintained a bullish stance, forecasting global demand growth of 1.3 million barrels per day (mb/d) for 2025. Conversely, the IEA's September report slightly upgraded its 2025 growth forecast to 740,000 bpd, citing resilient advanced economy demand but muted emerging market consumption. The EIA lowered its global demand growth estimate to 0.9 mb/d and, in its September Short-Term Energy Outlook, projected WTI spot prices to average $64.16/bbl in 2025 and drop to $47.77/bbl in 2026. Near-term sentiment is also supported by a weaker US dollar and high expectations of a Federal Reserve interest rate cut at its September 16–17 meeting, which could stimulate economic activity and fuel demand.
