WTI crude oil prices are currently hovering in the low $60s per barrel, reflecting a volatile and generally bearish trend under pressure from global trade tensions. Over the week, July WTI futures fluctuated, settling at $60.89 on May 27, rising to $61.84 on May 28, and settling at $60.94 on May 29. This volatility was heavily driven by a legal battle over U.S. tariffs; prices spiked briefly after a federal trade court blocked President Donald Trump's sweeping tariffs, but retreated after an appeals court temporarily reinstated them. Technically, WTI remains caught in a descending triangle consolidation pattern between $64.00 resistance and $56.25 support.

Market attention is highly focused on the upcoming OPEC+ virtual meeting, which has been rescheduled to Saturday, May 31, 2025. The "voluntary eight" member countries are expected to decide on July production levels, with strong anticipation of a 411,000 barrels per day (bpd) output hike. However, internal divisions persist; Russia's Deputy Prime Minister Alexander Novak has downplayed expectations of a quota increase, while Kazakhstan has openly resisted pressure to curb its overproduction, stating it does not intend to cut output.

Geopolitical and trade policy developments continue to dictate market sentiment. While President Trump's decision to delay proposed 50% tariffs on European Union imports to July 9 offered temporary relief, the threat of a broader trade war keeps demand expectations capped. On the geopolitical front, fresh U.S.-Iran nuclear talks in Rome concluded with little progress, suggesting that sanctions on Iranian oil exports will remain in place, providing a soft floor for prices. Additionally, supply risks are supported by a U.S. ban on Chevron exporting oil from Venezuela and localized production disruptions in Canada.

The global demand outlook remains subdued, weighed down by economic headwinds, tariff uncertainties, and reports of slowing consumption in China. In contrast, the U.S. domestic market is showing seasonal strength ahead of the summer driving season, with gasoline demand rising sharply to 9.45 million bpd from 8.64 million bpd the prior week. U.S. commercial crude inventories fell by 2.8 million barrels to 440.4 million barrels, leaving domestic stockpiles approximately 6% below the five-year average for this time of year.

Prominent financial institutions maintain a cautious outlook. Goldman Sachs Research recently reaffirmed its bearish stance, forecasting WTI to average $56 per barrel in 2025 and decline further to $52 per barrel in 2026, citing a projected surge in non-OPEC supply. Technical analysts point out that while WTI has staged brief breakouts above its descending trend lines, the commodity faces strong overhead resistance, with key Fibonacci retracement levels at $61.74 and $61.45 serving as immediate areas of dynamic support.
