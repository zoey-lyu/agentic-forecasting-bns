During the final week of October 2025, West Texas Intermediate (WTI) crude oil prices stabilized near the $61.00 to $61.80 per barrel range. This consolidation followed a volatile period where prices surged by approximately 5.5% on October 23 after the U.S. announced sweeping sanctions on major Russian oil firms. Despite this temporary geopolitical boost, WTI remains locked in a broader bearish trend established since late June, trading below its 200-day moving average and down roughly 5% month-on-month.

The primary headwind capping price gains is the expectation of rising supply from OPEC+. The alliance—led by Saudi Arabia and Russia—is moving forward with a scheduled production increase of 137,000 barrels per day (bpd) for November. This decision to continue unwinding voluntary cuts, despite a soft pricing environment, signals that the cartel is prioritizing reclaiming global market share over defending a price floor.

Geopolitical developments present a highly fluid supply outlook. The newly imposed U.S. sanctions targeting Russian energy giants Rosneft and Lukoil have stoked fears of tighter global supply, though the market is closely watching whether major Asian buyers like India will comply with Washington's pressure.

The demand outlook remains highly divided among major energy agencies. While OPEC maintains a relatively robust demand growth forecast of 1.3 million bpd for 2025, the IEA paints a far more bearish picture, projecting growth of just 700,000 bpd due to macroeconomic headwinds and accelerating vehicle electrification. Consequently, analysts at BloombergNEF and the IEA warn of a looming supply glut, with the global oil surplus projected to expand in 2025 and potentially exceed 4 million bpd by 2026 if OPEC+ continues its current production trajectory.
