WTI crude oil prices have shown tentative signs of stabilizing, trading around $60.88 per barrel as of November 12, 2025, after breaking above descending channel resistance near $60.04. This follows a period of bearish pressure where WTI closed at $59.75 on November 7, marking a multi-week decline driven by oversupply concerns. While technical indicators suggest a potential near-term recovery or consolidation within a bull flag pattern, the broader market remains subdued due to consecutive weekly builds in U.S. crude inventories and record domestic production.

In its Monthly Oil Market Report released on November 12, 2025, OPEC maintained its global oil demand growth forecasts, holding 2025 growth steady at 1.30 million barrels per day (bpd) and 2026 growth at 1.38 million bpd. This stability comes shortly after OPEC+ confirmed plans on November 2 to pause its scheduled voluntary production hikes during the first quarter of 2026 (January to March). Before this pause, the group will proceed with a modest 137,000 bpd increase in December, matching the increments set for October and November. However, the group's previous unwinding of cuts and ramping up to targets continue to weigh on prices, even as OPEC+ led a 440,000 bpd monthly decline in global supply in October.

Geopolitical tensions are providing a floor for prices amid escalating risks in key transit and production regions. In the Persian Gulf, Iran's seizure of an oil tanker in the Strait of Hormuz has renewed supply disruption fears. Meanwhile, the Russia-Ukraine conflict has intensified, with Ukrainian attacks targeting a major Russian export hub that handles approximately 2.2 million bpd (about 2% of global supply). Additionally, the U.S. is contemplating new sanctions on countries doing business with Russia and is planning to increase pressure on Venezuelan oil exports, further threatening global supply flows.

The demand outlook remains highly divided between major energy agencies. The International Energy Agency (IEA), in its November 13, 2025 report, noted that third-quarter global demand growth rebounded to 920,000 bpd, driven by stronger deliveries in China, but projected modest full-year growth of 790,000 bpd for 2025 and 770,000 bpd for 2026. Analysts note that while China's strategic stockpiling has absorbed some excess supply, a classic supply glut persists. Market forecasters warn that despite near-term geopolitical support, the combination of record non-OPEC+ output and OPEC+ unwinding is expected to keep the market heavily supplied heading into next year.
