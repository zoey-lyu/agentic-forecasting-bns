WTI crude oil prices have experienced high volatility but are showing signs of easing from their recent historic peaks. On April 15, 2026, NYMEX WTI crude for May delivery settled virtually unchanged at $91.29 per barrel, while ICE Brent for June delivery settled at $94.93 per barrel. By April 16, WTI was trading around $92.61 per barrel, recovering from an intraday low of $90.50. This represents a notable decline from late March when WTI hovered near $100 to $114 per barrel. The futures curve remains in an historically extreme state of backwardation; CME Group reported on April 16 that WTI contracts for December 2026 delivery are trading up to $40 below near-term May and June contracts, signaling that the market expects current supply disruptions to be resolved by year-end.

The primary driver of the market's risk premium is the ongoing conflict involving the U.S., Israel, and Iran, which began on February 28, 2026, and led to the near-total closure of the Strait of Hormuz. As of April 10, tanker traffic through this critical chokepoint remains severely restricted to just 5 to 7 vessels daily, compared to over 100 pre-war. However, geopolitical anxieties have softened following a temporary ceasefire announced on April 7 and a subsequent 10-point ceasefire proposal on April 8. Traders are closely monitoring upcoming diplomatic efforts, as White House and Iranian negotiators prepare for a second round of peace talks in Islamabad, Pakistan, which has introduced hopes of a negotiated end to the blockade.

The physical supply crunch from the conflict has manifested in unprecedented production drops. According to the International Energy Agency’s (IEA) April Oil Market Report released on April 14, global oil supply plummeted by 10.1 million barrels per day (bpd) in March to 97 million bpd. This historic disruption was led by OPEC+ production, which fell by 9.4 million bpd month-on-month to 42.4 million bpd, while non-OPEC+ supply declined by 770,000 bpd to 54.7 million bpd. Meanwhile, OPEC’s own Monthly Oil Market Report, released on April 13, noted that the OPEC Reference Basket (ORB) surged by $48.46 per barrel in March to average $116.36 per barrel due to these severe disruptions.

The demand outlook has sharply diverged among major energy agencies, with the gap between their 2026 projections widening to more than 1.5 million bpd. The IEA’s April report slashed its 2026 global demand forecast by 730,000 bpd from its previous estimate, now projecting a full-year contraction of 80,000 bpd. The IEA warns that a 1.5 million bpd demand decline in the second quarter of 2026 will be the sharpest since the COVID-19 pandemic, driven by demand destruction in the Middle East and Asia-Pacific. Conversely, OPEC continues to forecast robust demand growth for the year. In terms of technical forecasts, Rami Abu-Draa of Orbex noted on April 15 that WTI is holding above a key support zone of $84.20–$86.10, with immediate intraday support at $90.80. He projects that successful U.S.-Iran negotiations could push prices back toward the $101.00–$102.00 resistance zone, whereas a break below $84.10 could trigger a deeper drop toward $76.00.
