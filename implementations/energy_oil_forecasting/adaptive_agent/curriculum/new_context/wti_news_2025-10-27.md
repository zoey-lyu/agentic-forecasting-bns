Prior to the sanctions-driven rally, the market had been weighed down by OPEC+'s decision on October 5 to proceed with a modest production increase of 137,000 barrels per day starting in November 2025, representing a gradual unwind of voluntary cuts. Analysts note that OPEC's substantial spare capacity is expected to act as a buffer against a prolonged, one-sided price rally.

The demand outlook remains highly divided. The International Energy Agency (IEA) in its mid-October report projected subdued global demand growth of just 700,000 barrels per day for both 2025 and 2026, citing macroeconomic headwinds and accelerating vehicle electrification. Conversely, OPEC maintained a more optimistic forecast of 1.3 million barrels per day for 2025. In terms of market outlook, commodity analysts expect WTI to trade in a volatile range of $5 above or below $65 per barrel as the market balances geopolitical supply shocks against broader macroeconomic headwinds.
