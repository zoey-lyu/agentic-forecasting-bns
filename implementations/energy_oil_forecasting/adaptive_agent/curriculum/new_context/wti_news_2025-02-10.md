WTI crude oil futures experienced a volatile week, with March contracts settling at $71.03 per barrel on Wednesday, February 5, 2025, marking a $1.67 daily decline. Earlier in the week, WTI opened with bullish momentum on February 3 near $74.70 but quickly suffered a sharp drop of over 4% to the $72.00 zone. The commodity has entered a technical correction mode after breaking below its $72.50 support level, with prices testing immediate support around $70.50 to $70.60.

On February 3, 2025, the OPEC+ Joint Ministerial Monitoring Committee (JMMC) held its 58th meeting. The committee noted high conformity with production quotas for late 2024 and welcomed improved compliance and voluntary adjustments from Iraq and Kazakhstan. Overproducing members pledged to submit updated compensation schedules by the end of February. Russian Deputy Prime Minister Alexander Novak confirmed that several countries are still scheduled to begin gradually unwinding their voluntary production cuts starting April 1, 2025, phasing in the increases over an 18-month period.

Geopolitical and trade policy developments remain a primary driver of market uncertainty. While President Donald Trump delayed proposed 25% tariffs on Canadian and Mexican imports, the implementation of tariffs on China prompted immediate retaliation. Beijing responded by imposing duties on US exports, including crude oil, coal, and liquefied natural gas (LNG). This escalating trade friction, combined with doubts over whether a bilateral trade agreement can be reached before the February 10 deadline, has weighed heavily on market sentiment.

The demand outlook softened significantly following the release of the U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA) weekly petroleum status report on February 5. U.S. commercial crude inventories surged by 8.7 million barrels to 423.8 million barrels for the week ending January 31, vastly exceeding the consensus forecast of a 2 million to 2.4 million-barrel build. This massive build was attributed to refiners aggressively entering seasonal maintenance amid slack domestic gasoline demand. Gasoline stockpiles rose by 2.2 million barrels to 251.1 million barrels, while distillate inventories fell by 5.5 million barrels.

Market analysts highlight that the combination of rising inventories and trade tensions is keeping a lid on prices. John Kilduff of Again Capital noted that refiners currently have little immediate call for crude due to weak gasoline consumption. From a technical perspective, analysts suggest that WTI is facing a bearish path of least resistance, with critical resistance established at $74.50 and potential downside targets pointing toward a support floor between $65.00 and $67.00.
