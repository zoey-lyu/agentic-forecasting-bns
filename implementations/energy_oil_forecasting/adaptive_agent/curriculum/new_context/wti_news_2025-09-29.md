In late September 2025, West Texas Intermediate (WTI) crude oil prices staged a notable recovery. After testing a critical support zone of $61.45–$62.05 per barrel on September 19, WTI broke out of a descending technical pattern to clear the psychological $65.00 level, trading around $65.51 by September 25. Daily spot prices rose steadily through the week, climbing from $62.99 on Monday, September 22, to $65.51 on Thursday, September 25. This upward momentum was supported by a surprise reduction in U.S. crude inventories reported by the EIA, which helped offset broader macroeconomic concerns.

The market continues to digest OPEC+’s recent strategic pivot. On September 7, 2025, eight core members of the alliance (the "V8," including Saudi Arabia and Russia) agreed to increase oil production by 137,000 barrels per day (bpd) starting in October 2025. This decision followed a larger 547,000 bpd increase implemented in September as part of a gradual unwinding of voluntary cuts. Analysts note that by pushing ahead with the October increase rather than pausing, OPEC+ is signaling a clear ambition to prioritize reclaiming global market share over defending high prices, even at the risk of softer near-term pricing.

Geopolitical supply risks continue to provide a bullish floor for prices. Ongoing tensions in the Middle East, potential transit disruptions in the Strait of Hormuz, new U.S. sanctions on Iran, and the prolonged Russia-Ukraine conflict—including strikes on Russian refinery infrastructure—keep supply concerns active. However, these risks are balanced against a cautious global demand outlook. The IEA’s September report projected 2025 global demand growth at a modest 740,000 bpd, warning of a fourth-quarter contraction in OECD demand. Meanwhile, OPEC maintained a more optimistic demand growth forecast of 1.3 million bpd for 2025, though softening consumption in China remains a persistent headwind.

Prominent financial institutions expect downward pressure on prices heading into late 2025 and 2026 due to projected oversupply. J.P. Morgan forecasts WTI crude to average $63/bbl in 2025 and fall to $54/bbl in 2026, with Q4 2025 averaging $57/bbl. The U.S. EIA’s September Short-Term Energy Outlook projected Brent crude to average $59/bbl in Q4 2025 and drop to around $50/bbl in early 2026, driven by global inventory builds averaging over 2 million bpd through Q1 2026. Westpac similarly expects Brent to fall to $60/bbl by June 2026, reflecting a looming market surplus as non-OPEC+ supply continues to grow.
