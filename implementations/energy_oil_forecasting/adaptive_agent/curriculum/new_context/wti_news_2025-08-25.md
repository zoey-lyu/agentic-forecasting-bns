# WTI Market Context — 2025-08-25

WTI crude oil prices have experienced a dramatic reversal from their summer peaks, tumbling back toward the low $60s per barrel as geopolitical risk premiums fade. On Tuesday, August 19, WTI fell over 1% to close at $62.35 per barrel, its lowest level since late May, amid optimism over potential Russia-Ukraine peace talks. However, prices rebounded on Wednesday, August 20, with WTI rising 1.4% to settle at $63.21 per barrel. This recovery was driven by a highly bullish weekly report from the U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA), which revealed that domestic crude inventories fell by 6.014 million barrels for the week ending August 15—more than triple the 1.8 million-barrel draw expected by analysts. WTI has extended these modest gains into Thursday, August 21, trading around $62.86 to $63.00 per barrel.

The downward pressure on prices is heavily reinforced by OPEC+ actively restoring barrels to the market. The eight OPEC+ nations participating in voluntary cuts—including Saudi Arabia and Russia—agreed to increase production by 548,000 barrels per day (bpd) in August, accelerating the phased rollback of their 2.2 million bpd voluntary supply cuts. According to the International Energy Agency (IEA), these higher targets, which continue into September, are expected to boost global oil supply growth to 2.5 million bpd in 2025, with non-OPEC+ producers contributing 1.3 million bpd of that growth.

Geopolitical supply risks have shifted from the Middle East to Eastern Europe and trade policy. The risk premium from the Israel-Iran conflict has largely dissipated following a June ceasefire, leaving the Strait of Hormuz open and global supply chains uninterrupted. Instead, traders are monitoring the Russia-Ukraine conflict; a recent Ukrainian drone attack on Russia's Druzhba oil pipeline system temporarily halted flows, and another drone strike triggered a fire near the Kursk Nuclear Power Plant, highlighting ongoing infrastructure vulnerabilities. Additionally, trade tensions are escalating as the U.S. prepares to impose a 25% tariff on Indian goods starting August 27 in response to India's continued purchases of discounted Russian crude.

The global demand outlook remains mixed. On one hand, U.S. summer demand is showing robust signs, with gasoline stocks dropping by 2.7 million barrels last week and jet fuel consumption climbing to its highest four-week average since 2019. On the other hand, broader global demand is slowing. The IEA's August report downgraded its 2025 global demand growth forecast to 680,000 bpd (down from 700,000 bpd) due to weaker-than-expected consumption in China, India, and Brazil. Conversely, OPEC's August Monthly Oil Market Report maintained a more optimistic but unchanged global demand growth forecast of 1.29 million bpd for 2025, driven primarily by non-OECD nations.

Market analysts are increasingly aligning on a bearish outlook for the next 18 months due to a projected supply glut. In its August Short-Term Energy Outlook, the EIA sharply downgraded its WTI spot price forecast, projecting WTI to average $63.58 per barrel in 2025 and plummet to $47.77 per barrel in 2026. J.P. Morgan expects WTI to average $63 per barrel this year and $54 next year, while Fitch's BMI is slightly more conservative, forecasting WTI at $65 in 2025 and $64 in 2026. Standard Chartered remains a bullish outlier, predicting WTI will average $58 in 2025 before jumping to $75 in 2026. Highlighting the shifting sentiment, advisory firm AEGIS officially moved its crude oil view to bearish on August 21, citing the high probability of a rapid rise in global inventories.
