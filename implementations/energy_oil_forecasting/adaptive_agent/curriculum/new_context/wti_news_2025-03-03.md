WTI crude oil prices have experienced a downward trend, falling below the key psychological level of $70 per barrel. On February 25, 2025, WTI for April delivery dropped by 2.5% ($1.77) to settle at $68.93 per barrel, a level that held through February 27. This represents a decline of nearly 5% from the start of the month. Technical analysts noted that WTI broke below its descending wedge support around $70, trading below both its 100-day and 200-day simple moving averages, which confirms a near-term bearish outlook.

OPEC+ production plans continue to weigh on market sentiment, as the alliance remains on track to implement a scheduled production hike in April. This prospect has revived oversupply concerns among traders. In its February Monthly Oil Market Report, OPEC maintained a relatively bullish demand growth forecast of 1.85 million barrels per day (b/d) for 2025, whereas the International Energy Agency (IEA) projected a more modest growth of 1.1 million b/d, driven primarily by China's petrochemical sector and emerging Asian economies.

Geopolitical developments are exerting downward pressure on prices. Recent signs of progress in Russia-Ukraine peace talks have raised expectations that Russian crude could flow more freely back into global markets, easing supply constraints. Additionally, the market is grappling with trade policy uncertainties. While potential U.S. tariffs on Canadian and Mexican energy imports had previously lent some support to WTI by threatening supply lines, broader concerns over retaliatory trade measures and tariff-induced economic slowdowns are now dampening the demand outlook.

The demand outlook has softened significantly due to weak macroeconomic indicators. In the U.S., the Conference Board reported that consumer confidence plunged by 7 points in February to 98.3—its sharpest monthly drop in three and a half years—while the Expectations Index fell to 72.9, a level historically indicative of an impending recession. Similar economic weakness in Germany has further stoked energy demand concerns. In response, market analysts are adopting a cautious stance; for instance, some forecast WTI to remain capped within a $67.50 to $77.50 range for March, citing poor consumer sentiment and tariff uncertainties as major headwinds.
