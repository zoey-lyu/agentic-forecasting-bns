WTI crude oil prices have experienced a steady downward trajectory, sliding to multi-month lows amid a growing consensus of a looming supply surplus. By mid-December, WTI was trading between $56 and $58 per barrel, settling at $57.44 on December 12 and hovering around $56.15 by December 18. Brent crude similarly slumped, slipping below the $60 per barrel threshold for the first time since May. Bearish sentiment has been compounded by thin holiday trading and a significant rise in short positioning, pushing crude toward the bottom of its recent trading range.

In its monthly report released on December 11, OPEC maintained its global oil demand growth forecasts at 1.3 million barrels per day (bpd) for 2025 and 1.4 million bpd for 2026. To support the market, OPEC+ confirmed its decision to suspend planned production increases through the first quarter of 2026, while retaining the flexibility to gradually unwind 1.65 million bpd of voluntary cuts if conditions permit. However, OPEC raised its forecast for non-OPEC+ supply growth in 2025 to 1 million bpd, citing rising output from the United States, Canada, Brazil, and Argentina.

Geopolitical factors are driving mixed market signals. Downward pressure on prices has intensified due to optimism surrounding Russia-Ukraine peace negotiations, with market participants anticipating that a potential ceasefire could eventually ease sanctions and restore Russian crude flows. Conversely, downside risks are being partially offset by escalating U.S. actions against Venezuela. The Trump administration recently announced new sanctions targeting Venezuelan shipping assets, including six oil tankers, following the seizure of a sanctioned supertanker, which has raised concerns over near-term supply disruptions.

The demand outlook remains a point of divergence among major energy watchdogs. While OPEC remains optimistic, the International Energy Agency (IEA) projected more modest global demand growth of 830,000 bpd for 2025 and 860,000 bpd for 2026. The IEA warned of an impending, unprecedented global surplus, pointing out that global observed inventories climbed to a four-year high of 8,030 million barrels in October. From a technical perspective, market analysts note that WTI is locked in a descending triangle pattern with a critical horizontal support floor at $55.75; a failure to hold this level could trigger a sharp technical selloff.
