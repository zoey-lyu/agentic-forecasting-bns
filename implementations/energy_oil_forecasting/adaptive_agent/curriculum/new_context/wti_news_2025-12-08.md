West Texas Intermediate (WTI) crude oil is trading around $59.21 to $59.67 per barrel as of December 4, 2025, showing a modest recovery from a late-November low. Throughout November, WTI experienced a controlled downdraft, declining approximately 4.0% over the month from $60.98 to finish near $58.52, with a monthly average settlement of $59.47 per barrel. This downward pressure has kept WTI consolidating within a descending triangle pattern, repeatedly testing horizontal support near $55.99 to $57.50 per barrel while facing a falling resistance line that has capped recent rallies. Brent crude has moved in tandem, trading near $62.93 per barrel.

The OPEC+ alliance concluded its 40th Ministerial Meeting on November 30, 2025, reaffirming overall crude production targets through December 31, 2026. Crucially, the eight participating countries—including Saudi Arabia, Russia, and the UAE—reconfirmed their plan to implement a minor production increase of 137,000 barrels per day (bpd) in December 2025, but will pause all subsequent monthly increments for January, February, and March 2026 due to seasonal demand weakness. Additionally, the alliance approved a new mechanism to assess members' maximum sustainable production capacity to establish 2027 baselines, while receiving updated compensation schedules from overproducing members to ensure strict quota compliance.

Geopolitical supply risks and macroeconomic factors continue to inject volatility into the market. Key concerns include ongoing Middle East tensions, shipping disruptions, and U.S. sanctions targeting major Russian oil producers like Lukoil and Rosneft. On the demand side, however, the market is weighed down by a highly cautious outlook. Sluggish economic activity and soft demand from major importing regions, particularly China, have fostered an oversupply narrative, with comfortable global inventories and repeated upside surprises in U.S. crude stocks keeping prices under pressure.

Market analysts suggest that WTI remains technically vulnerable to further losses if it fails to break above its descending resistance, with immediate support levels identified around $55.99 per barrel. A downside breakout would signal a continuation of the longer-term decline, whereas a strong break above the descending resistance line could trigger a rally toward the $65.00 to $70.00 range. Looking ahead, major financial institutions expect a soft pricing environment, with J.P. Morgan projecting WTI to average $65 per barrel in the final quarter of 2025 before dropping to an average of $60 per barrel in the first quarter of 2026.
