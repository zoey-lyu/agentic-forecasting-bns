West Texas Intermediate (WTI) crude oil prices have drifted lower over the past week, reversing a strong mid-month rally that had pushed prices toward $80 per barrel. On Wednesday, January 22, 2025, WTI crude futures settled down at $75.44 a barrel, and continued their slide on Thursday, January 23, to finish down 1.09% at $74.62 a barrel on the NYMEX. This downward pressure is partly attributed to momentum-focused speculators liquidating long positions after net longs in Brent and WTI futures had reached an April 2024 high of 470,000 contracts earlier in the month.

The OPEC+ alliance has maintained its cautious stance, having previously postponed its production revival plans. In its January 2025 Monthly Oil Market Report (MOMR), OPEC kept its 2025 global oil demand growth forecast at approximately 1.3 to 1.4 million barrels per day (mb/d). However, the group faces immediate political pressure; on January 23, newly inaugurated U.S. President Donald Trump delivered a speech at the World Economic Forum in Davos, Switzerland, calling on Saudi Arabia and OPEC to lower crude oil prices, which immediately weighed on market sentiment.

Geopolitical supply risks have eased following a ceasefire agreement between Israel and Hamas, which has alleviated immediate fears of major supply disruptions in the Middle East. Conversely, physical oil and tanker markets continue to experience friction from the U.S. sanctions imposed on Russia's oil sector on January 10. Domestically, a severe winter storm temporarily shut in 130,000 to 160,000 barrels per day of North Dakota's oil production. Meanwhile, the Energy Information Administration (EIA) reported on January 23 that U.S. crude oil inventories fell by 1.0 million barrels for the week ending January 17, leaving stocks at 411.7 million barrels—about 6% below the five-year average.

The global demand outlook is increasingly clouded by trade policy uncertainties. Following his inauguration, President Trump declared a national energy emergency to accelerate domestic drilling ("drill, baby, drill") and threatened sweeping tariffs, including a 25% tariff on Canada and Mexico and a 10% tariff on China. Analysts warn that these trade barriers could significantly dampen global economic growth and oil consumption. Technical analysts note that WTI has broken below its ascending trend line support, signaling a potential near-term bearish trend toward $73.00, while commodity strategists highlight that while Russian sanctions cause short-term tightness, the broader market is adjusting to expectations of higher U.S. supply and tariff-induced demand destruction.
