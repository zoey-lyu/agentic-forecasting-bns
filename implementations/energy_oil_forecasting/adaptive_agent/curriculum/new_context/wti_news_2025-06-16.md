WTI crude oil prices have experienced significant volatility, staging a strong rally during the week. After trading around $64.58 on June 6 and hovering near $65.16 on June 10, WTI surged over 4% on June 11 to reach its highest level since early April. On June 12, prices eased slightly, with WTI settling around $67.92 per barrel. This upward trend was supported by technical indicators, such as WTI breaking above its $64.00 resistance level and a bullish moving average crossover.

OPEC+ is continuing its plan to gradually unwind voluntary production cuts. The alliance is scheduled to increase oil production by 411,000 barrels per day in July, marking the fourth consecutive month of unwinding. This follows a similar 411,000 bpd production adjustment implemented for June. While some analysts express concern that the rapid restoration of idled capacity could lead to a supply glut later in the year, others suggest that rising domestic oil demand within OPEC+ nations, particularly Saudi Arabia, could offset the additional supply and support prices.

Geopolitical tensions in the Middle East remain the primary driver of the recent price spike. Concerns over potential supply disruptions intensified after the U.S. decided to move military and diplomatic personnel out of the region, with President Donald Trump warning that the Middle East "could be a dangerous place" and reiterating that Iran would not be allowed to develop a nuclear weapon. Although no direct threat to oil infrastructure has been identified, the prospect of conflict has injected a significant geopolitical risk premium into the market. Additionally, the European Union is proposing a new round of sanctions against Russia, including a potential lowering of the Russian oil price cap from $60 to $45 per barrel, adding further supply-side uncertainty.

The demand outlook is heavily influenced by macroeconomic developments, particularly ongoing U.S.-China trade negotiations in London, where progress has temporarily eased trade-related downside risks for the world's two largest oil consumers. However, the U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA) released a cautious forecast, projecting that global supply will eclipse demand by 800,000 barrels per day in 2025—the largest surplus since it began forecasting for the year. The EIA also expects the downward pressure on crude prices to persist through 2025 and 2026 as global production outpaces demand, though it notes that lower prices are already beginning to curb U.S. supply growth, with domestic production projected to dip slightly in 2026.
