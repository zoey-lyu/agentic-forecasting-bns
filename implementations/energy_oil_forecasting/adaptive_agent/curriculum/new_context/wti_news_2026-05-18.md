**Price Level and Recent Trend**
West Texas Intermediate (WTI) crude oil futures have surged back above the $100 threshold, with front-month contracts trading between $101.02 and $102.32 per barrel as of May 13–14, 2026. This represents a gain of more than 12% over the last month. The price rally has been reignited as market participants price out prior optimism for a swift diplomatic resolution to the Middle East crisis. Meanwhile, Brent crude is trading near $110.87 per barrel, keeping the Brent-WTI spread wide at over $8 per barrel due to localized shipping disruptions and elevated U.S. inventory levels capping domestic gains.

**OPEC+ Production and Geopolitical Supply Risks**
The de facto closure of the Strait of Hormuz due to the U.S.-Iran conflict continues to severely choke global supply, with over 14 million barrels per day (bpd) of Gulf production currently shut in. According to OPEC’s monthly report released on May 13, the cartel's crude production plummeted by 1.7 million bpd in April to 18.98 million bpd. Saudi Arabia’s output fell to 6.316 million bpd—its lowest level since the 1990 Persian Gulf War—while Kuwaiti production plunged by 561,000 bpd to just 600,000 bpd. Total OPEC+ alliance output fell by 1.74 million bpd to 33.19 million bpd. Geopolitical tensions remain high after President Trump rejected Iran's latest peace proposal as "totally unacceptable" on May 11, keeping a heavy risk premium embedded in prices.

**Demand Outlook**
The severe supply shock has triggered significant demand destruction, leading major agencies to downgrade their outlooks in their May 13 reports. However, their projections remain highly polarized. OPEC revised its 2026 global oil demand growth forecast down to 1.17 million bpd (from 1.38 million bpd previously), expecting total consumption to reach 106.3 million bpd. Conversely, the International Energy Agency (IEA) projects a full-year global demand contraction of 420,000 bpd (down from its previous estimate of an 80,000 bpd decline) to 104 million bpd. S&P Global has also cut its 2026 demand growth forecast by 700,000 bpd due to the war. Despite falling demand, the IEA warns the market will face a massive 1.78 million bpd deficit for the full year of 2026, with a Q2 undersupply as severe as 6 million bpd.

**Notable Analyst Forecasts**
In its first oil price publication in two months, J.P. Morgan released its updated projections on May 13. The bank's analysts, led by Natasha Kaneva, forecast that WTI spot prices will average $89 per barrel in 2026 and $70 per barrel in 2027. For the global Brent benchmark, J.P. Morgan projects an average of $96 per barrel in 2026 and $75 per barrel in 2027. These targets sit below some competing bank forecasts from earlier in the spring, such as Morgan Stanley and Citigroup, which both targeted a Q2 Brent average of $110 per barrel.
