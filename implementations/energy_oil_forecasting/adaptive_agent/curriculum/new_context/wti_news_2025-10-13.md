West Texas Intermediate (WTI) crude oil prices settled at $60.88 per barrel on Friday, October 3, 2025, capping a sharp weekly loss of 7.4% driven by expectations of rising global supply. By mid-week on October 8, WTI experienced a modest rebound to trade above $62 per barrel, while Brent crude hovered near $66 per barrel. Despite this minor daily uptick, the broader trend remains bearish, with both benchmarks recently touching four-month lows as the market transitions from third-quarter stability toward an anticipated surplus.

On October 5, 2025, eight key members of the OPEC+ alliance—including Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iraq, and the UAE—held a virtual meeting and agreed to proceed with a scheduled production increase of 137,000 barrels per day (bpd) for November. This decision continues the gradual unwinding of the 1.65 million bpd voluntary cuts established in April 2023. While Saudi Arabia has pushed to increase output to reclaim market share, the alliance emphasized a cautious approach, reiterating that they retain full flexibility to pause or reverse these production increases if market conditions deteriorate.

Geopolitical risks continue to inject volatility and maintain a risk premium in the market. In Eastern Europe, stalled peace efforts in Ukraine and ongoing drone strikes on Russian refineries and fuel depots have constrained Russia's domestic processing capacity, forcing a surge in its unprocessed crude exports to near 16-month highs. In the Middle East, a fragile ceasefire agreement between Israel and Hamas has temporarily eased immediate supply disruption fears. However, supply side pressures are mounting elsewhere, as the Iraq-Turkey (Kurdish) pipeline is expected to resume flows after a two-year shutdown.

The demand outlook is increasingly weighed down by seasonal factors, as summer driving demand in the Atlantic Basin comes to an end. Although recent U.S. data showed a short-term uptick in fuel consumption and a 1.8-million-barrel draw at the Cushing, Oklahoma hub, nationwide crude inventories are expected to rise. Analysts from JPMorgan and Rystad Energy warn that the market has reached a turning point, with an oversupplied balance expected to dominate the fourth quarter of 2025 and beyond. Additionally, BOK Financial analysts noted that the combination of rising OPEC+ supply and the Kurdish pipeline restart will likely keep near-term sentiment bearish.
