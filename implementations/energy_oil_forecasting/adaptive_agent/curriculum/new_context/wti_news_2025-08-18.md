WTI crude oil prices have experienced downward pressure, falling below the $63 per barrel threshold. On August 13, 2025, WTI crude futures settled at $62.65 per barrel, while Brent crude settled at $65.63 per barrel. This decline represents a drop of approximately 13% for crude prices since the start of the year, driven by thin summer trading and a bearish outlook. The immediate catalyst for the mid-August slide was a surprise 3-million-barrel increase in U.S. crude inventories to 426.7 million barrels, signaling that the peak summer driving season is winding down and refiners are beginning to scale back output.

On the supply side, OPEC+ output fell by 230,000 barrels per day (b/d) in July, though this was offset by rising non-OPEC+ production. However, prices have been heavily pressured by OPEC+ plans to fully unwind its 2.2 million b/d voluntary output cuts starting in September. In its August 12 Monthly Oil Market Report (MOMR), OPEC maintained its global oil demand growth projection for 2025 but revised its 2026 demand growth estimate upward by 100,000 b/d to 1.38 million b/d. Meanwhile, the U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA) projected that U.S. crude production will reach a record 13.41 million b/d in 2025 due to improved well productivity, before declining in 2026 in response to lower prices.

Geopolitical developments are heavily influencing market sentiment, with traders adopting a "wait-and-see" approach ahead of a highly anticipated meeting between U.S. President Donald Trump and Russian President Vladimir Putin in Alaska. The talks are aimed at negotiating a ceasefire to end the war in Ukraine, which could potentially lead to an easing of U.S. sanctions on Russian oil exports. However, the White House has tempered expectations for a swift peace agreement, a factor that continues to inject caution and provide some underlying support to prices against a broader bearish backdrop.

The demand outlook remains soft, as highlighted by the International Energy Agency (IEA) in its August 13 report. The IEA projected global oil demand growth of just 680,000 b/d for 2025 and 700,000 b/d for 2026, citing weaker-than-expected consumption in China, India, and Brazil. With global supply growth projected by the IEA at 2.5 million b/d in 2025 and 1.9 million b/d in 2026, analysts warn of a looming supply surplus and a potential record glut next year. Market analysts, such as Keshav Lohiya of Oilytics, emphasize that the market is currently in a holding pattern, balancing these bearish supply-demand fundamentals against the fluid geopolitical landscape.
