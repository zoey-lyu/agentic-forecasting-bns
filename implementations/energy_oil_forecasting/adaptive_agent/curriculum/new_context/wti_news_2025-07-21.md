West Texas Intermediate (WTI) crude oil prices have drifted lower, trading between $66.00 and $66.81 per barrel as of mid-July 2025, while Brent crude has hovered near $68.86 per barrel. This downward movement extends a multi-day losing streak, reversing earlier gains. The price slide has been driven by easing geopolitical tensions and recent U.S. government data showing falling distillate demand alongside rising crude inventories at the key storage hub in Cushing, Oklahoma.

According to OPEC’s Monthly Oil Market Report released in mid-July, the group's crude production rose by 263,000 barrels per day (bpd) to reach 27.54 million bpd, led by a 170,000 bpd increase from Saudi Arabia to 9.53 million bpd. Total OPEC+ production climbed by 335,000 bpd to 41.94 million bpd. Market participants are increasingly focused on the prospect of oversupply later this year as OPEC+ moves forward with plans to fast-track the return of previously halted supplies.

Geopolitical supply anxieties have softened following a tempered stance from the White House. U.S. President Donald Trump’s announcement of a 50-day grace period for Russia to end the conflict in Ukraine has been interpreted by markets as a temporary reprieve from immediate, harsher sanctions, calming near-term supply jitters. However, broader trade risks remain on the horizon, as the administration has hinted at a potential 30% tariff on imports from the EU and Mexico starting in August, raising concerns about global economic friction and its impact on energy demand.

The demand outlook remains highly divided between major agencies. The IEA’s July 11 report forecast global oil demand growth to rise by just 700,000 bpd in 2025—the lowest rate since 2009, excluding 2020—citing lacklustre emerging market consumption. Conversely, OPEC maintained a more optimistic forecast, holding its 2025 demand growth projection steady at 1.3 million bpd. Amid these dynamics, Goldman Sachs recently raised its Brent price outlook for the second half of 2025 but expressed caution for 2026, while other analysts describe the current market as rangebound, with peak seasonal demand providing a floor against waning geopolitical risk.
