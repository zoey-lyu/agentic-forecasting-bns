**Price Level and Recent Trend**
WTI crude oil prices have experienced significant volatility over the past week, driven by a sharp escalation in Middle East geopolitical tensions. Following Israeli airstrikes on Iranian military and nuclear infrastructure on June 13, 2025, WTI front-month futures surged from around $67 per barrel to a session close high of $76.28, with some intraday spikes reaching as high as $77.62. Since that initial surge, the market has entered a consolidation phase. As of June 19, 2025, WTI is trading in a range between $74.86 and $75.67 per barrel, reflecting a persistent risk premium even as some immediate profit-taking has capped further upward momentum.

**Geopolitical Supply Risks**
The primary catalyst for the recent price spike is the direct military confrontation between Israel and Iran. Market participants are highly focused on the potential for supply disruptions in the Persian Gulf, particularly regarding the Strait of Hormuz. Because approximately 20% of the world's liquid petroleum passes through this narrow choke point, any closure or severe disruption would trigger a massive global supply shock. While some regional producers are reportedly accelerating shipments to move crude onto floating tankers before shipping lanes are compromised, and analysts suggest a total blockade remains unlikely due to Iran's reliance on exports to China and India, the threat of escalation keeps the market on edge.

**OPEC+ Production Decisions and Supply Outlook**
This geopolitical crisis comes at a time when OPEC+ is transitioning its supply strategy. According to OPEC’s Monthly Oil Market Report (MOMR) released on June 16, 2025, the group’s average production in May rose by 180,000 barrels per day (bpd) to 41.23 million bpd, reflecting the initial stages of unwinding voluntary production cuts. Prior to the June 13 airstrikes, this rising supply—coupled with robust non-OPEC production growth from the U.S., Brazil, and Canada—had kept prices depressed, with WTI averaging just $60.94 per barrel in May. The International Energy Agency (IEA) noted in its June 17 report that global supply rose by 330,000 bpd in May to 105 million bpd, and the ongoing unwinding of OPEC+ cuts is expected to ensure comfortable balances for the second half of the year.

**Demand Outlook and Analyst Forecasts**
The demand outlook remains a point of divergence between major forecasting agencies. In its June 17 report, the IEA marginally trimmed its 2025 global oil demand growth forecast to 720,000 bpd, pointing to weak second-quarter deliveries in the U.S. and China. Conversely, OPEC maintained its more bullish demand growth forecast of 1.3 million bpd for both 2025 and 2026, citing resilient economic growth in non-OECD countries, particularly India and China. Market analysts observe that while technical indicators show WTI consolidating in overbought territory due to war-related headlines, the underlying fundamentals of expanding non-OPEC supply and ample OPEC+ spare capacity should eventually cap long-term price gains once geopolitical tensions subside.
