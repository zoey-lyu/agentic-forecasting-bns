# WTI Market Context — 2025-12-01

West Texas Intermediate (WTI) crude oil prices have experienced a downward trend, trading near $58.19 to $58.67 per barrel in late November 2025. The broader market sentiment remains soft, with the WTI-Brent spread hovering around $4.12 per barrel in favor of Brent. This softening trend was recently exacerbated by a surprise build in U.S. commercial crude inventories, which rose by 2.77 million barrels for the week ending November 21, defying market expectations of a 2.33 million barrel draw. Despite this build, overall U.S. inventories remain relatively low compared to the five-year average.

The OPEC+ alliance is navigating a delicate balance between supporting prices and managing supply. The eight core member nations—Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iraq, the UAE, Kuwait, Kazakhstan, Algeria, and Oman—previously agreed to a modest production increase of 137,000 barrels per day (bpd) for November and December 2025. However, to counter seasonal demand weakness, the group has decided to pause these monthly output hikes during the first quarter of 2026 (January through March). The alliance is scheduled to meet virtually on November 30, 2025, to review global market conditions and reaffirm this strategy.

Geopolitical risks continue to influence market sentiment, though recent developments have eased some supply fears. Reports of progress in U.S.-brokered peace talks between Russia and Ukraine have exerted downward pressure on prices, offsetting concerns from sporadic Ukrainian drone strikes on Russian energy infrastructure. On the demand side, the outlook remains cautious. In its November 2025 Monthly Oil Market Report, OPEC projected global oil demand growth of 1.3 million bpd for 2025 and 1.4 million bpd for 2026, driven almost entirely by non-OECD economies. However, this demand is being met with robust non-OPEC supply growth, particularly from the U.S., Brazil, Canada, and Argentina, leading to oversupply concerns as global crude exports reached near-record highs of 46 million bpd in October.

Financial institutions expect WTI prices to remain under pressure through 2026 due to this expanding supply. In its November 2025 Short-Term Energy Outlook (STEO), the U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA) raised its WTI spot price forecasts, projecting WTI to average $65.15 per barrel in 2025 and $51.26 per barrel in 2026. The EIA expects WTI to average $58.65 per barrel in the fourth quarter of 2025, before dropping to $50.30 per barrel in the first quarter of 2026 and averaging $52.00 per barrel in the second half of 2026. Other major institutions generally align with this downward trajectory, citing the interaction between robust non-OPEC supply and slowing demand growth.
