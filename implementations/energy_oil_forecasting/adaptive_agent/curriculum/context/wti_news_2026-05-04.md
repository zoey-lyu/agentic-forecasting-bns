### Price Level and Recent Trend
In late April 2026, West Texas Intermediate (WTI) crude oil futures hovered around the critical psychological level of $100 per barrel, trading at approximately $100.46. This capped a dramatic 63% rally over nine weeks from a February low of $61.35, driven by the outbreak of the U.S.-Iran war. Prices peaked at a war-related high of $119.86 earlier in the month before experiencing extreme volatility. On April 17, 2026, WTI and Brent prices plummeted by roughly 9% following an announcement from Tehran that the Strait of Hormuz would temporarily reopen during a ceasefire. However, the relief was short-lived as the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) quickly re-closed the passage, keeping a heavy geopolitical risk premium embedded in crude benchmarks.

### OPEC+ Production Decisions
During a virtual meeting on April 5, 2026, eight OPEC+ member nations (including Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iraq, and the UAE) agreed to implement a production increase of 206,000 barrels per day (bpd) starting in May. This adjustment was intended to gradually unwind the 1.65 million bpd voluntary cuts first enacted in April 2023. Saudi Arabia and Russia were scheduled to drive over 60% of this increment, each adding 62,000 bpd. However, market analysts widely dismissed the move as "symbolic" or "academic" because the physical crude remained trapped behind conflict zones. This decision came on the heels of a staggering collapse in OPEC's crude output in March—falling by 7.56 million bpd to 22 million bpd—marking the largest monthly decline in at least four decades.

### Geopolitical Supply Risks
The dominant driver of the oil market was the severe geopolitical crisis in the Middle East. Following U.S. and Israeli airstrikes on Iran, the Strait of Hormuz—which typically handles a fifth of global oil supply—was effectively blocked starting February 28, 2026. The Energy Information Administration (EIA) estimated that Persian Gulf producers collectively shut in 7.5 million bpd of crude in March, with shut-ins projected to rise to 9.1 million bpd in April. Supply risks were further compounded on April 8 when a key Saudi backup pipeline, designed to divert up to 7 million bpd to the Red Sea, was struck by militants. Additionally, the U.S. initiated a naval blockade of Iranian ports on April 13, severely tightening global physical balances.

### Demand Outlook and Analyst Forecasts
The severe supply shock led to diverging demand outlooks among major energy agencies. In its April 14 Oil Market Report, the International Energy Agency (IEA) drastically downgraded its outlook, forecasting global oil demand to contract by 80,000 bpd on average in 2026 (down from a previously expected growth of 730,000 bpd) due to war-induced demand destruction and high prices. Conversely, OPEC maintained a more robust full-year demand growth forecast of 1.4 million bpd, despite lowering its second-quarter projection by 500,000 bpd due to "transitory weakness". In terms of forecasts, the EIA's April Short-Term Energy Outlook projected Brent to average $96/bbl in 2026, assuming the conflict would resolve and traffic would gradually resume. Meanwhile, a Reuters poll of analysts doubled the projected 2026 global oil deficit to 1.5 million bpd, warning that current prices did not yet fully reflect the extreme physical tightness of the market.
