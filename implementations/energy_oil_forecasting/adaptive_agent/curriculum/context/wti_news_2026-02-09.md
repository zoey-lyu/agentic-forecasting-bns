In late January and early February 2026, West Texas Intermediate (WTI) crude oil prices showed short-term resilience, hovering primarily in the $60 to $62 per barrel range. During the week ending January 30, 2026, WTI prices jumped 4.1% to settle at $62.52 per barrel, up from $60.06 the prior week. This late-January rally was supported by a combination of severe winter weather in the United States, tightening physical supply of heavy and residual barrels, and a resurgence of geopolitical risk premiums. However, this upward momentum stood in contrast to a broader, structurally bearish sentiment that dominated the market heading into the new year.

To counter seasonal demand weakness and maintain market stability, OPEC+ took a defensive stance. During virtual meetings on January 4 and February 1, 2026, eight key member nations—including Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iraq, the UAE, Kuwait, Kazakhstan, Algeria, and Oman—reaffirmed their decision to pause planned production increases through March 2026. Under this agreement, production quotas remain frozen at December 2025 levels, keeping Saudi Arabia’s target at 10.103 million barrels per day (mb/d) and Russia’s at 9.574 mb/d. Additionally, the alliance continued to pressure overproducing members, receiving updated compensation plans from Iraq, the UAE, Kazakhstan, and Oman to offset excess volumes produced since early 2024.

Tightening distillate markets, exacerbated by sanctions on Russian and Iranian flows, further supported refined product margins and kept a risk premium embedded in front-month crude contracts.

The demand outlook for 2026 presents a mixed picture among major energy agencies. In its January 2026 Oil Market Report, the International Energy Agency (IEA) revised its global oil demand growth forecast upward by 69,000 barrels per day (bpd) to 930,000 bpd, citing a normalization of economic conditions. However, the IEA warned that overall growth would remain tempered by sub-par global GDP, rising electric vehicle adoption, and energy efficiency gains.
