As of mid-May 2026, West Texas Intermediate (WTI) crude oil prices are trading near the $100 per barrel mark, closing the week ending May 17, 2026, at approximately $100.65 per barrel. Prices have maintained a structurally bullish trend and heightened volatility since late February, driven by an unprecedented geopolitical supply shock. Notably, the Brent-WTI spread has widened significantly—averaging around $12 per barrel in March—as elevated U.S. inventory levels and strategic stockpiling cap domestic WTI gains relative to the international benchmark.

The dominant driver of the global energy market is the ongoing Strait of Hormuz crisis, which began on February 28, 2026, following the outbreak of the U.S.-Iran conflict. In retaliation for military actions, Iran blockaded the Strait of Hormuz, a critical maritime chokepoint that typically handles over 20% of global seaborne oil trade. The U.S. simultaneously blockaded Iranian ports starting in mid-April. Recent diplomatic efforts, including a U.S.-China summit and proposals mediated by Pakistan, have failed to yield a resolution, keeping a substantial geopolitical risk premium embedded in crude prices.

This blockade has severely disrupted Middle Eastern oil flows, making it impossible for OPEC+ to deliver on its planned April output increases. The U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA) estimates that key Gulf producers collectively shut in 10.5 million barrels per day (b/d) of crude production in April, with OPEC+ output alone falling by 1.74 million b/d during the month. Adding to the group's shifting dynamics, the UAE officially exited OPEC on May 1, 2026, to free itself from production quotas, though its immediate export capacity remains constrained by the ongoing Hormuz blockade.

The economic fallout of the war and soaring fuel costs have led major energy agencies to downgrade their global demand outlooks. In its May 13, 2026 report, the International Energy Agency (IEA) forecast global oil demand to contract by 420,000 b/d year-over-year in 2026 to 104 million b/d, representing a 1.3 million b/d drop from pre-war projections. Similarly, OPEC's May Monthly Oil Market Report revised its 2026 global demand growth forecast down to 1.17 million b/d (from 1.38 million b/d), citing the conflict's severe impact on global trade flows and fuel consumption.

Market analysts remain highly focused on the duration of the Hormuz closure. In its latest Short-Term Energy Outlook (STEO), the EIA raised its full-year 2026 Brent spot price forecast to $95–$96 per barrel and WTI to $87.41 per barrel, assuming a gradual resumption of shipping traffic starting in June. Meanwhile, Goldman Sachs revised its Q4 2026 Brent forecast upward to $90 per barrel. Analysts warn that while massive strategic petroleum reserve (SPR) releases and commercial inventory draws have temporarily cushioned the supply shock, these buffers are rapidly depleting, leaving the market highly vulnerable to prolonged disruptions.
