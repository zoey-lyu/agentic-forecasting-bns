WTI crude oil prices have experienced a dramatic surge and heightened volatility in early March 2026. After trading in the low-to-mid $60s per barrel during January and February, WTI prices spiked sharply, piercing above $90 per barrel in early March. This rapid escalation followed a month-on-month increase of $4.26 per barrel in February, as reported in the OPEC Monthly Oil Market Report, and has been driven entirely by sudden, severe geopolitical developments in the Middle East.

In response to evolving market conditions, the eight OPEC+ countries (Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iraq, UAE, Kuwait, Kazakhstan, Algeria, and Oman) met virtually on March 1, 2026. Moving away from their previous production freeze that was maintained through March, the coalition decided to begin gradually unwinding the 1.65 million barrels per day (bpd) of voluntary cuts first announced in April 2023. They agreed to implement a production increase of 206,000 bpd starting in April 2026. However, the group emphasized a cautious approach, retaining full flexibility to pause or reverse these adjustments if market volatility persists.

The primary catalyst for the oil market's upheaval is the outbreak of military conflict in the Middle East. Following US-Israeli airstrikes on Iran starting February 28, 2026, and subsequent regional retaliations, shipping traffic through the Strait of Hormuz—which handles roughly 20% of global oil trade—has ground to a near-total standstill. The International Energy Agency (IEA) on March 12, 2026, characterized this as the largest supply disruption in the history of the global oil market, noting that Gulf countries have cut total production by at least 10 million bpd. To mitigate the crisis, IEA member countries agreed on March 11 to release 400 million barrels of oil from emergency reserves.

The geopolitical shock has significantly altered the demand outlook. While the IEA initially projected global oil demand to rise by 850,000 bpd in 2026, its March 12 report warned that widespread flight cancellations and LPG supply disruptions in the Middle East will curb global demand by approximately 1 million bpd during March and April. Analysts note that soaring prices and a precarious global economic outlook present further downside risks to consumption. Market forecasters remain highly focused on the duration of the Strait of Hormuz blockade, warning that while non-OPEC+ producers are expected to drive a projected 1.1 million bpd average supply increase for the year, near-term prices will remain extremely volatile with a strong upward bias as long as the chokepoint remains closed.
