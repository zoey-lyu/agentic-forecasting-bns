As of mid-April 2026, global crude oil markets are experiencing extreme volatility and trading at multi-year highs following the most severe oil supply shock in history. The Brent crude spot price averaged $103 per barrel in March, peaking near $128 per barrel on April 2. West Texas Intermediate (WTI) crude oil futures have similarly surged, with market sentiment and prediction platforms pricing in a high probability of WTI testing or exceeding the $120 per barrel mark during April. This dramatic upward trajectory represents the largest monthly price gain on record, completely reversing the pre-conflict environment of oversupply and falling prices.

The primary driver of this price surge is a massive geopolitical supply disruption in the Middle East. Following joint U.S. and Israeli air strikes on Iran on February 28, 2026, the critical Strait of Hormuz was effectively closed to shipping traffic. This chokepoint normally handles roughly 20% of global oil supply. Consequently, global oil supply plummeted by an unprecedented 10.1 million barrels per day (mb/d) in March. Supply risks escalated further on April 8 when a key Saudi Arabian pipeline—which was being utilized to bypass the Strait and divert up to 7 million bpd to the Red Sea—was struck and damaged just hours after a temporary ceasefire was announced.

In response to these tight market conditions, OPEC+ has made incremental adjustments, though analysts view them as largely symbolic. During virtual meetings on March 1 and April 5, 2026, the group decided to proceed with gradually unwinding its voluntary cuts, agreeing to production increases of 206,000 barrels per day for both April and May respectively. However, because the Strait of Hormuz remains blocked, much of this physical crude remains trapped behind the conflict zone. Industry surveys indicate that OPEC's actual crude output collapsed by 7.56 mb/d in March (a 25% drop) as regional producers were forced to shut in massive volumes of production.

The demand outlook has fractured as high prices and supply scarcity begin to trigger demand destruction. In its April 14 Oil Market Report, the International Energy Agency (IEA) slashed its 2026 global oil demand growth forecast by 730,000 bpd, now projecting a contraction of 80,000 bpd for the full year. The IEA warns of a sharp 1.5 mb/d demand decline in the second quarter, particularly in the Middle East and Asia-Pacific regions. Conversely, OPEC has maintained its robust demand growth projections, creating a stark contrast with both the IEA and the U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA), the latter of which halved its demand outlook in its April 7 report. To cushion the historic supply deficit, IEA member countries have coordinated the largest strategic petroleum reserve (SPR) release in history, totaling 426 million barrels, with the U.S. contributing over 172 million barrels.
