In early March 2026, West Texas Intermediate (WTI) crude oil prices surged dramatically, with futures jumping past $80 and topping $90 per barrel in early March sessions. This sharp rally followed a relatively stable February, during which WTI front-month contracts averaged around $64.50 per barrel. The sudden price spike has been accompanied by record trading volumes and a steepening forward curve, pushing the market into deep backwardation as prompt physical barrels trade at historically large premiums to outer-month futures. 

The primary driver of this market shock is the outbreak of intense military conflict in the Middle East involving the United States, Israel, and Iran. On February 28, 2026, military strikes led to the effective closure of the Strait of Hormuz—a critical maritime chokepoint responsible for the transit of approximately 20% of global seaborne oil. The International Energy Agency (IEA) has characterized this as the largest supply disruption in the history of the global oil market, projecting a plunge in global oil supply of 8 million barrels per day (mb/d) in March. To mitigate the crisis, IEA member countries unanimously agreed on March 11 to release 400 million barrels of oil from emergency reserves.

Prior to the escalation, OPEC+ had maintained strict production discipline, reaffirming voluntary output cuts and production pauses through March 2026 (including Saudi Arabia holding production at approximately 10.1 mb/d and Russia at 9.6 mb/d) to prioritize market stability. However, the sudden closure of the Strait of Hormuz has severely constrained Gulf producers' ability to export, forcing shut-ins and drastically altering the group's actual output. While non-OPEC+ producers in the Atlantic Basin and North America are expected to provide some supply relief, they cannot fully offset the massive Middle Eastern curtailments.

The demand outlook has diverged significantly between major energy agencies. In its March 12 report, the IEA downgraded its 2026 global oil demand growth forecast by 210,000 barrels per day (b/d) to 640,000 b/d, citing the economic fallout of the war and reducing its March and April demand projections by over 1 mb/d. Conversely, OPEC’s March 11 report held its 2026 global demand growth forecast steady at 1.4 mb/d. Analysts warn that the market faces extreme volatility and a severe supply deficit if the Hormuz closure persists, raising the specter of a 1970s-style energy crisis characterized by global inflation, panic buying, and heightened recessionary risks.
